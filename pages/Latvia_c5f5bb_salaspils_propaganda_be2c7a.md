## Introduction

Salaspils occupies a difficult place in [Latvia]({{ 'how-latvias-most-famous-hoaxes-fooled/' | relative_url }})’s history because two truths exist at the same time. First, Salaspils was a real Nazi camp where thousands of people suffered through forced labour, hunger, disease, imprisonment without trial and the deaths of many adults and children. Second, Soviet authorities later reshaped that history into a political narrative that dramatically inflated victim numbers and promoted claims that could not be supported by historical evidence. The result was not a simple hoax but a propaganda story built around genuine atrocities. Understanding Salaspils therefore requires separating documented Nazi crimes from later exaggerations without diminishing either the suffering of prisoners or the importance of historical accuracy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salaspils_camp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Salaspils camp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Salaspils camp</span></span></span>

This case is one of Latvia’s most significant examples of how propaganda can distort public memory. Rather than inventing an event that never happened, Soviet narratives took a real site of suffering and transformed it into a symbol designed to serve broader ideological goals.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://militaryheritagetourism.info/en/military/sites/view/48" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: militaryheritagetourism.info">[Military Heritage Tourism]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">militaryheritagetourism.info</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This repressive camp was a part of...Read more...</span></span></span>

## What Is Securely Known About Imprisonment and Death at Salaspils

Salaspils was established by Nazi authorities near Riga during the German occupation of Latvia. It functioned as a police prison, forced-labour camp and detention centre for a changing population of prisoners, including political detainees, civilians caught in anti-partisan operations, Jews involved in the camp’s construction, and others considered undesirable by the occupation regime. Conditions were brutal. Prisoners faced inadequate food, disease, harsh labour and physical punishment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salaspils_camp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Salaspils camp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Salaspils camp</span></span></span>

Modern historical research has substantially revised earlier estimates of the camp’s scale. Historians working with archival evidence have concluded that tens of thousands of people passed through Salaspils over the course of its existence, but not the hundreds of thousands sometimes claimed during the Soviet era. Recent scholarship places the number of prisoners at roughly 23,000 and the number of deaths at more than 3,000, while other major studies estimate around 2,000–3,000 deaths. Regardless of the exact figure, the death toll was severe and reflected the lethal conditions inside the camp.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scienceopen.com/book?vid=a10b9675-4303-43b2-bc01-106efb17eb47" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scienceopen.com">[scienceopen.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scienceopen.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Salaspils Camp in Latvia, 1941-1944The Salaspils camp near Riga, which was operated during the Nazi occupation of Latvia...</span></span></span>

One of the most tragic aspects of Salaspils involved children. Large numbers of children arrived from regions devastated by German anti-partisan operations, particularly in Belarus and nearby territories. Disease, malnutrition and neglect killed many of them. Archaeological and documentary evidence confirms substantial child mortality, making the suffering of children a central part of the camp’s history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salaspils_camp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Salaspils camp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Salaspils camp</span></span></span>

The secure historical picture, therefore, is already grim. There is no need to exaggerate the record to establish that Salaspils was a place of profound human suffering under Nazi occupation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salaspils_camp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Salaspils camp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Salaspils camp</span></span></span>

## How Soviet Narratives Enlarged Numbers and Simplified Victimhood

After the Second World War, Soviet authorities incorporated Salaspils into a wider narrative about fascist crimes and Soviet liberation. In this framework, the camp became not merely a historical site but a political symbol. Soviet publications and memorial culture often presented Salaspils as a place where more than 100,000 people had been murdered, a figure that modern historians regard as unsupported by evidence. Contemporary scholarship and Latvian historical research have identified these numbers as products of Soviet-era propaganda rather than archival reality.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salaspils_camp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Salaspils camp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Salaspils camp</span></span></span>

Another widely circulated claim portrayed Salaspils as a site where enormous numbers of children were systematically drained of blood for wounded German soldiers. Historians have found evidence that blood samples were taken from some child prisoners, but the sweeping Soviet narrative transformed scattered incidents and genuine suffering into a dramatic story presented as a defining feature of the camp. The propaganda version often eclipsed more complex realities about disease, neglect, forced labour and the varied prisoner population.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.apgads.lu.lv/fileadmin/user_upload/lu_portal/apgads/PDF/Konferences/2023/ms22/ms22-02-Birzniece.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apgads.lu.lv">[APGads]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apgads.lu.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Censorship and Self-Censorship in the Letters of Salaspils...by M Birzniece — camp, such as mortality of children, blood sampling...</span></span></span>

The Soviet interpretation also tended to merge different categories of victims into a single story of “peaceful Soviet citizens,” downplaying distinctions that modern historians regard as important. Jewish prisoners, political detainees, deportees from Belarus and other groups were frequently absorbed into a simplified narrative that emphasised Soviet collective martyrdom and anti-fascist heroism.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cultures-of-history.uni-jena.de/exhibitions/a-dual-legacy-reviewing-the-new-exhibition-at-salaspils" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cultures-of-history.uni-jena.de">[Cultures of History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cultures-of-history.uni-jena.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">a dual legacy reviewing the new exhibition at salaspils</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Germans quickly replaced Latvian workers with Jews deported to Riga from the German...Read more...</span></span></span>

This simplification served political purposes. By highlighting Nazi crimes in a particular way, Soviet authorities could reinforce the legitimacy of Soviet rule while drawing attention away from crimes committed by Stalin’s regime. Salaspils became part of a broader memory landscape in which historical suffering was organised into a state-approved narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://militaryheritagetourism.info/en/military/stories/view/192" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: militaryheritagetourism.info">[Military Heritage Tourism]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">militaryheritagetourism.info</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Military Heritage TourismAbout the Salaspils Memorial as a symbol of the ideology...The description vividly describes the extent to whic...</span></span></span>

## Why the Distorted Story Was Persuasive

Propaganda about Salaspils succeeded partly because it rested on a foundation of truth. Unlike an invented atrocity story, it referred to a real camp where people genuinely died and suffered. That reality made inflated claims harder to challenge. When audiences already knew that terrible crimes had occurred, extraordinary numbers and dramatic allegations could appear plausible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salaspils_camp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Salaspils camp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Salaspils camp</span></span></span>

The memorial culture surrounding the site reinforced this impression. The monumental Salaspils memorial complex, opened during the Soviet period, presented the camp as a powerful symbol of fascist brutality. Its architecture and symbolism encouraged emotional engagement, often without inviting detailed examination of disputed statistics or historical nuances.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://eng.lsm.lv/article/culture/history/german-traces-in-latvia-salaspils-memorial-ensemble.a444542/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eng.lsm.lv">[LSM.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eng.lsm.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">german traces in latvia salaspils memorial ensemble.a444542</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">german traces in latvia salaspils memorial ensemble.a444542</span></span></span>

The closed nature of the Soviet information system also mattered. Independent archival research was limited, competing interpretations were discouraged, and official publications often repeated the same claims. Repetition gave disputed figures an appearance of authority.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://militaryheritagetourism.info/en/military/stories/view/192" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: militaryheritagetourism.info">[Military Heritage Tourism]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">militaryheritagetourism.info</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Military Heritage TourismAbout the Salaspils Memorial as a symbol of the ideology...The description vividly describes the extent to whic...</span></span></span>

## How Historians Re-examined the Evidence

The collapse of Soviet control opened archives and allowed researchers in Latvia and abroad to investigate the camp using documentary records, demographic analysis and comparative studies of Nazi detention systems. Historians including Kārlis Kangeris and others associated with the Latvian historians’ commissions undertook detailed examinations of prisoner numbers, camp administration and mortality records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://eng.lsm.lv/article/culture/history/new-book-attempts-real-history-of-holocaust-camp.a166107/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eng.lsm.lv">[LSM.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eng.lsm.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">book attempts real history of Holocaust campThe book gives an account of events at Salaspils based on historical evidence rather than que...</span></span></span>

Their findings did not minimise Nazi crimes. Instead, they sought to establish what could be demonstrated from evidence. Research showed that many Soviet-era claims could not be substantiated and that the frequently repeated death tolls were far higher than surviving records justified. At the same time, scholars documented the harsh conditions, child mortality, forced labour and widespread suffering that unquestionably occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://eng.lsm.lv/article/culture/history/new-book-attempts-real-history-of-holocaust-camp.a166107/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eng.lsm.lv">[lsm.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eng.lsm.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">book attempts real history of Holocaust campThe book gives an account of events at Salaspils based on historical evidence rather than que...</span></span></span>

The resulting picture is more complicated than either Soviet propaganda or later political polemics sometimes suggest. Salaspils was neither the gigantic extermination centre described in some Soviet accounts nor a minor detention facility whose crimes can be dismissed. It was a brutal Nazi camp whose history was subsequently reshaped for political ends.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salaspils_camp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Salaspils camp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Salaspils camp</span></span></span>

## Why Correcting Propaganda Must Not Erase Genuine Nazi Crimes

One of the recurring dangers in debates about Salaspils is the tendency to swing from one extreme to another. Because Soviet authorities exaggerated certain aspects of the camp’s history, some commentators have tried to use those exaggerations to cast doubt on the reality of Nazi crimes there. Historians reject this reasoning. The exposure of false numbers does not make the suffering less real.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://eng.lsm.lv/article/culture/history/new-book-attempts-real-history-of-holocaust-camp.a166107/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eng.lsm.lv">[LSM.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eng.lsm.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">book attempts real history of Holocaust campThe book gives an account of events at Salaspils based on historical evidence rather than que...</span></span></span>

The challenge is to hold two facts together. Soviet propaganda distorted the record, inflating figures and simplifying complex histories. Yet the camp still imprisoned thousands of people under appalling conditions, caused thousands of deaths and left deep scars on survivors and their families. Correcting the propaganda is therefore an act of historical clarification, not historical denial.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salaspils_camp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Salaspils camp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Salaspils camp</span></span></span>

Salaspils remains an important lesson in how political systems can manipulate collective memory. The most durable distortions are often not complete inventions but narratives that blend truth with exaggeration. Because real suffering lies at their core, they can survive for decades and continue shaping public understanding long after the original political circumstances have disappeared.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/26765326" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jstor.org">[jstor.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jstor.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on jstor.org.</span></span></span>
