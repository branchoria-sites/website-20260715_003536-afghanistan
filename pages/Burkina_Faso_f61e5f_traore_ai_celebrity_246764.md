## Introduction

By 2025, misinformation about [Burkina Faso]({{ 'how-burkina-fasos-online-legends-were/' | relative_url }})'s military leader Ibrahim Traoré had moved beyond recycled photographs and mislabelled videos. A new wave of artificial-intelligence content presented him as a figure admired by some of the world's most recognisable celebrities, religious leaders and entertainers. The central claim was simple: famous people supposedly saw Traoré as an extraordinary statesman and publicly praised him. The evidence, however, was fabricated.

AI-generated speeches, deepfake videos and synthetic songs portrayed international figures as enthusiastic supporters of the Burkinabè leader. Among the most widely shared examples were a fake message attributed to Pope Leo XIV and invented songs supposedly performed by celebrities including R. Kelly. These productions were not minor internet jokes. They accumulated millions of views, crossed language barriers and helped build an online mythology in which Traoré appeared to enjoy near-universal admiration from global cultural and moral authorities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://factcheck.afp.com/doc.afp.com.48HX3T2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: factcheck.afp.com">[AFP Fact Check]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">factcheck.afp.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AFP Fact CheckVideo showing Pope Leo XIV praising Burkina Faso&#x27;s...30 May 2025 — Pope Leo XIV is the latest public figure targeted by pe...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2025</span></span></span>

## The Altered Pope Leo XIV Endorsement

One of the most influential examples emerged shortly after the election of Pope Leo XIV in 2025. Viral videos claimed to show the new pope delivering a personal message to Traoré, praising his leadership, condemning colonialism and celebrating Burkina Faso's achievements. The recordings were presented as official Vatican communications and were widely shared across Facebook, TikTok, YouTube and messaging platforms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://factcheck.afp.com/doc.afp.com.48HX3T2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: factcheck.afp.com">[AFP Fact Check]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">factcheck.afp.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AFP Fact CheckVideo showing Pope Leo XIV praising Burkina Faso&#x27;s...30 May 2025 — Pope Leo XIV is the latest public figure targeted by pe...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2025</span></span></span>

Fact-checkers, Vatican officials and independent analysts quickly demonstrated that the videos were false. The footage came from genuine Vatican appearances by the pope, but the audio had been replaced with AI-generated speech and the visuals altered to create the illusion that he was speaking directly about Burkina Faso. Vatican News publicly stated that the supposed message was fabricated, while digital-forensics specialists identified signs of deepfake manipulation, including mismatched lip movements and synthetic voice generation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vaticannews.va/en/world/news/2025-05/pope-leo-xiv-burkina-faso-fake-video-message.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vaticannews.va">[vaticannews.va]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vaticannews.va</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pope leo xiv burkina faso fake video message</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vatican NewsFake message to President of Burkina Faso attributed to...21 May 2025 — A 36-minute speech in English, created with artifici...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2025</span></span></span>

What made the deception effective was not technical perfection. Many versions contained obvious flaws. Instead, the video's power came from the authority attached to the speaker. For many viewers, a favourable statement from the head of the Catholic Church appeared to provide independent moral validation of Traoré's image as a defender of African sovereignty. The prestige of the pope became part of the message itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://factcheck.afp.com/doc.afp.com.48HX3T2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: factcheck.afp.com">[AFP Fact Check]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">factcheck.afp.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AFP Fact CheckVideo showing Pope Leo XIV praising Burkina Faso&#x27;s...30 May 2025 — Pope Leo XIV is the latest public figure targeted by pe...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2025</span></span></span>

## Fake Songs and Praise Attributed to International Stars

The celebrity myth expanded beyond politics and religion into popular culture. Social-media users circulated AI-generated songs supposedly created by famous musicians in honour of Traoré. These productions often combined synthetic vocals with dramatic images of the Burkinabè leader addressing crowds, reviewing troops or standing beside national symbols.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://factcheck.afp.com/doc.afp.com.46MR7KD" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: factcheck.afp.com">[AFP Fact Check]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">factcheck.afp.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kelly and Burkina Faso&#x27;s interim leader Ibrahim Traore while a song plays in the background.Read more...</span></span></span>

One of the most prominent cases involved a song falsely attributed to R. Kelly. Posts claimed that the imprisoned American singer had composed and dedicated an emotional tribute to Traoré. AFP's investigation found no evidence that Kelly had produced the song. Instead, the recording showed clear signs of AI generation, with synthetic vocals used to imitate the artist's voice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://factcheck.afp.com/doc.afp.com.46MR7KD" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: factcheck.afp.com">[AFP Fact Check]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">factcheck.afp.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kelly and Burkina Faso&#x27;s interim leader Ibrahim Traore while a song plays in the background.Read more...</span></span></span>

The phenomenon soon widened. Reports documented fabricated content featuring supposed praise from figures such as Beyoncé and Rihanna, while other viral posts suggested that major international celebrities were discussing Traoré's achievements or celebrating his political vision. None of these endorsements were authentic. They were products of AI image, audio and video tools that allowed anonymous creators to manufacture celebrity approval at minimal cost.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/firstpostin/posts/firstpostafrica-burkina-fasos-ibrahim-traore-gets-ai-praise-from-beyonc%C3%A9-pope-ai/1236429198518065/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[facebook.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on facebook.com.</span></span></span>

Unlike traditional political propaganda, these creations often resembled fan tributes rather than official government messaging. They blended music-video aesthetics, inspirational storytelling and celebrity culture into a format designed for algorithm-driven platforms. The result was a new type of political mythmaking in which entertainment and propaganda became difficult to separate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newlinesmag.com/reportage/africas-ai-strongmen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newlinesmag.com">[New Lines Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newlinesmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">africas ai strongmen</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">africas ai strongmen</span></span></span>

## Why Borrowed Celebrity Authority Worked

The success of these fabrications depended on a familiar psychological mechanism: borrowed authority.

Most viewers have limited direct knowledge of Burkina Faso's politics. When a globally recognised figure appears to endorse a leader, some people treat that endorsement as a shortcut for evaluating credibility. The celebrity's reputation is transferred to the political figure. Whether the celebrity is a pope, musician or actor matters less than the symbolic message that "important people support this person".<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://factcheck.afp.com/doc.afp.com.48HX3T2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: factcheck.afp.com">[AFP Fact Check]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">factcheck.afp.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AFP Fact CheckVideo showing Pope Leo XIV praising Burkina Faso&#x27;s...30 May 2025 — Pope Leo XIV is the latest public figure targeted by pe...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2025</span></span></span>

The strategy was particularly effective because Traoré had already become a prominent symbol within wider pan-African discussions. Many supporters viewed him as a representative of resistance to foreign influence and as a successor to the legacy of Thomas Sankara. In that environment, fabricated celebrity praise often reinforced beliefs that audiences already wanted to be true. Rather than creating admiration from nothing, the deepfakes amplified existing hopes and political sentiments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2025/08/16/burkina-faso-traore-russia-disinformation/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: washingtonpost.com">[washingtonpost.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">washingtonpost.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Washington Post How a little-known coup leader became a Pan-African hero</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">His government has launched programs like local food production, infrastructure development, and electric vehicle ambitions, winning supp...</span></span></span>

The content also travelled far beyond Burkina Faso. Researchers and journalists observed that AI-generated tributes gained audiences across Anglophone and Francophone Africa, within diaspora communities, and among social-media users who had little previous interest in Burkinabè affairs. Celebrity faces provided a familiar entry point into a political narrative that might otherwise have attracted limited attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lemonde.fr/afrique/article/2025/06/30/burkina-faso-ibrahim-traore-superstar-de-l-anti-imperialisme-online_6617007_3212.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lemonde.fr">[Le Monde.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lemonde.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">burkina faso ibrahim traore superstar de l anti imperialisme online 6617007 3212</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">burkina faso ibrahim traore superstar de l anti imperialisme online 6617007 3212</span></span></span>

## How Investigators Exposed the Myth

The celebrity myth was not uncovered through a single dramatic revelation. Instead, investigators repeatedly applied ordinary verification techniques.

Fact-checkers compared viral videos with original recordings, revealing that genuine footage had been altered. They traced source material back to Vatican broadcasts, examined audio inconsistencies and consulted specialists in synthetic media. In the case of the pope videos, both church sources and technical experts concluded that the speeches had been fabricated with AI tools.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vaticannews.va/en/world/news/2025-05/pope-leo-xiv-burkina-faso-fake-video-message.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vaticannews.va">[vaticannews.va]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vaticannews.va</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pope leo xiv burkina faso fake video message</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vatican NewsFake message to President of Burkina Faso attributed to...21 May 2025 — A 36-minute speech in English, created with artifici...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2025</span></span></span>

For the fake songs, investigators looked for evidence that the artists had released the recordings through official channels. No such evidence existed. ## From Political Rumour to Digital Legend

The significance of the AI celebrity myth lies less in any single fake video than in the cumulative effect of hundreds of similar productions. Together they created an alternative narrative in which Traoré appeared to be admired by religious leaders, musicians, entertainers and public figures around the world. The repetition itself became persuasive.

For students of hoaxes and misinformation, the episode marks a notable stage in Burkina Faso's digital folklore. Earlier propaganda often depended on distorting reality. The newer generation of AI content simply invented it. Celebrity endorsements, songs and speeches did not need to happen; software could create them on demand.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nos.nl/artikel/2566956-dankzij-ai-is-leider-burkina-faso-een-hype-op-social-media-hij-heeft-de-vibe" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nos.nl">[nos.nl]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nos.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nos.nl.</span></span></span>

The result was a modern political legend built not from forged documents or fabricated newspaper stories, but from synthetic voices and digital performances. Even after individual videos were debunked, the broader image they promoted often survived.
