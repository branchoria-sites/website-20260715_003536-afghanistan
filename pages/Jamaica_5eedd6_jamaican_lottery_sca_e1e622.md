## Introduction

The Jamaican lottery scam is not a genuine lottery at all. It is a form of advance-fee fraud in which callers tell victims they have won a large cash prize, a luxury car, a holiday or a sweepstakes jackpot, then demand payments to release the supposed winnings. The promised prize never exists. What makes the scam notable is not merely the false claim but the way it traps people through repeated payments, emotional pressure and carefully constructed trust. For more than a decade, Jamaican-based lottery fraud networks have targeted thousands of victims overseas, particularly older adults in the United States and Canada, causing losses ranging from a few hundred pounds to entire life savings. Investigations by Jamaican and international authorities show that the scam relies on persuasion, impersonation and persistence rather than sophisticated technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://jm.usembassy.gov/lottery-scams/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jm.usembassy.gov">[usembassy.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jm.usembassy.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lottery Scams</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Embassy in JamaicaThe most prevalent in Jamaica is the lottery scam, where scammers lead victims to believe they have won a drawing or lo...</span></span></span>

## How the False Prize Scheme Works

At its core, the scam begins with an unexpected phone call. The victim is told that they have won a substantial prize through a lottery, sweepstakes or promotion they may never have entered. The caller claims to represent a well-known organisation, government office or prize company and congratulates the target on their good fortune.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ag.state.mn.us/consumer/publications/JamaicanLotteryScam.asp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ag.state.mn.us">[mn.us]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ag.state.mn.us</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Jamaican Lottery ScamJamaican fraudsters claiming that they&#x27;ve won large cash prizes, vacation getaways, vehicles, or other prizes. The catch appears almost immediately. Before the prize can be delivered, the winner must supposedly pay:

* Insurance or processing charges.
* Customs fees.

The amount requested is often presented as relatively small compared with the enormous prize being promised. A victim who believes they are about to receive hundreds of thousands or even millions may see a payment of a few hundred or a few thousand pounds as a reasonable hurdle. Victims can become trapped in a cycle of escalating demands that lasts months or even years.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.moca.gov.jm/what-we-investigate" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: moca.gov.jm">[moca.gov.jm]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">moca.gov.jm</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">What We InvestigatePopularly known as the &#x27;lottery scam&#x27;, advanced fee fraud preys on both local and international victims. ### They Use Real Personal Information

Scammers often know a target's name, address, telephone number or other personal details. Information gathered from public records, marketing lists or previous data leaks can make the call sound legitimate. A victim may conclude that only a genuine organisation could possess such details.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2009/06/ftc-urges-consumers-report-jamaica-based-telemarketing-scams" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ftc.gov">[Federal Trade Commission]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ftc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Trade CommissionFTC Urges Consumers to Report Jamaica-based...Jun 26, 2009 — The rip-off artists pose as lawyers, customs offici...</span></span></span>

### They Borrow Authority

Callers frequently impersonate lawyers, customs officials, lottery representatives or government employees. Some victims receive forged documents, official-looking letters or certificates bearing seals and logos intended to reinforce the deception.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2009/06/ftc-urges-consumers-report-jamaica-based-telemarketing-scams" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ftc.gov">[Federal Trade Commission]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ftc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Trade CommissionFTC Urges Consumers to Report Jamaica-based...Jun 26, 2009 — The rip-off artists pose as lawyers, customs offici...</span></span></span>

### They Build Relationships

Unlike many internet scams, lottery fraud can involve repeated conversations over weeks or months. Some callers cultivate what appears to be a friendship, checking in regularly and discussing family matters. This personal connection can make the eventual requests for money seem more believable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://people.com/lottery-scam-cost-elderly-woman-home-money-11976341" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: people.com">[People.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">people.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">She Thought She&#x27;d Won the Lottery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Instead, a Scam Cost an Elderly Woman Her Home and $600KA 73-year-old woman from southwest Washington was scammed out of over $600,000 an...</span></span></span>

### They Exploit Commitment

Once a victim has sent one payment, they may feel psychologically committed. Admitting the prize is fictional means admitting the earlier payment was lost. Scammers exploit this tendency by continually presenting the next fee as the final obstacle before the winnings arrive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.moca.gov.jm/what-we-investigate" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: moca.gov.jm">[moca.gov.jm]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">moca.gov.jm</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">What We InvestigatePopularly known as the &#x27;lottery scam&#x27;, advanced fee fraud preys on both local and international victims. ## How Victims Become Trapped

The most damaging cases often involve elderly victims who are contacted repeatedly over long periods. Law-enforcement investigations have documented people losing retirement savings, borrowing against their homes and sending money dozens of times because they remain convinced that a life-changing prize is close at hand.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sheriffs.org/jamaica-sweepstakes-fraud-targets-older-people/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sheriffs.org">[sheriffs.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sheriffs.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sheriffs.org.</span></span></span>

One recent prosecution involved a Jamaican citizen who falsely told a 73-year-old woman that she had won a multimillion-dollar prize. Over several years, he persuaded her to send more than US$600,000 in supposed taxes and fees. Prosecutors stated that the demands started small and steadily increased until the victim had sold her home, yet no prize ever existed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://people.com/lottery-scam-cost-elderly-woman-home-money-11976341" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: people.com">[People.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">people.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">She Thought She&#x27;d Won the Lottery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Instead, a Scam Cost an Elderly Woman Her Home and $600KA 73-year-old woman from southwest Washington was scammed out of over $600,000 an...</span></span></span>

Some fraud networks go further by maintaining contact through multiple telephone numbers, sending supporting paperwork and even using intermediaries to collect funds. In certain cases, former victims have unknowingly been recruited to move money for the scammers, helping conceal the organisers behind the scheme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ctinsider.com/journalinquirer/article/east-hartford-woman-charged-feds-lottery-scam-20254385.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ctinsider.com">[CT Insider]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ctinsider.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ctinsider.com.</span></span></span>

## Why the Scam Became Associated with Jamaica

The lottery scam is not unique to [Jamaica]({{ 'jamaica/' | relative_url }}), but Jamaican-based networks became particularly notorious during the 2000s and 2010s. Investigations by American and Jamaican authorities identified organised groups operating largely from the Montego Bay area and other locations, using international telephone calls to reach victims abroad.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/jamaican-national-pleads-guilty-defrauding-elderly-americans-through-jamaica-based-lottery" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[justice.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Jamaican National Pleads Guilty to Defrauding Elderly...Aug 19, 2022 — Greg Warren Clarke, 29, of Montego Bay, Jamaica, pleaded guilty t...</span></span></span>

The fraud attracted significant international attention because of its scale. Authorities reported thousands of victims and millions of dollars in losses. Several major investigations resulted in extraditions, convictions and prison sentences in both Jamaica and the United States.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/contact-us/field-offices/minneapolis/news/press-releases/jamaican-man-sentenced-to-prison-for-involvement-in-international-lottery-fraud-scheme" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[fbi.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on fbi.gov.</span></span></span>
