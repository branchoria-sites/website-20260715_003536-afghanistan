## Introduction

[Cyprus]({{ 'when-cyprus-stories-became-more/' | relative_url }}) has its share of stories that sit in a grey area where belief, folklore and possible misunderstanding overlap. Unlike a forged antiquity or a deliberate fraud, some claims are difficult to classify because no clear evidence of deception has ever emerged. Reports of weeping religious icons and tales of a mysterious sea creature near Ayia Napa have persisted for decades, attracting pilgrims, tourists, media attention and sceptical scrutiny alike. The central question is not simply whether these stories are true or false. It is how extraordinary claims gain credibility, why they endure despite limited evidence, and what kind of proof would be needed to resolve them. In Cyprus, these cases reveal how uncertainty itself can become part of a story's appeal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spokesman.com/stories/1997/feb/10/weeping-icon-draws-thousands-tears-seen-as/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spokesman.com">[spokesman.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spokesman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Review Weeping Icon Draws Thousands Tears Seen As Miracle,</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ReviewWeeping Icon Draws Thousands Tears Seen As Miracle...February 10, 1997 — 10 Feb 1997 — Many Greek Cypriots regard the 4...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 10, 1997</span></span></span>

## Hoax, Folklore or Honest Misinterpretation?

Not every famous claim belongs neatly in the category of hoax. A hoax normally involves deliberate deception. Many Cypriot miracle reports and monster legends are better understood as examples of contested evidence. Witnesses may be sincere, observers may disagree about what they saw, and later retellings may exaggerate events without any identifiable fraudster behind them.

This distinction matters because it changes the historical question. Instead of asking who faked the event, investigators ask whether natural explanations can account for the reports, whether memories changed over time, and whether social or economic incentives encouraged the story to spread. In Cyprus, the most famous examples are reports of icons apparently shedding tears and the enduring legend of the so-called Ayia Napa sea monster.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.cyprus-mail.com/2000/12/31/weeping-skies-keep-the-faithful-away-from-crying-icon/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.cyprus-mail.com">[Cyprus Mail Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.cyprus-mail.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">weeping skies keep the faithful away from crying icon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">But why they appear to come from the Virgin&#x27;s eyes no one can explain.Read more...</span></span></span>

## What Witnesses Claimed to See

### The Weeping Icons

Reports of weeping icons have appeared periodically in Cyprus, particularly within the Orthodox Christian tradition. One of the best-known episodes occurred in the 1990s when icons associated with the Kykkos area were reported to be producing tears or an oily liquid. News coverage described worshippers travelling to view images said to be weeping from the eyes of the Virgin Mary and Christ. Some believers interpreted the phenomenon as a divine warning or a sign of coming events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spokesman.com/stories/1997/feb/10/weeping-icon-draws-thousands-tears-seen-as/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spokesman.com">[spokesman.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spokesman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Review Weeping Icon Draws Thousands Tears Seen As Miracle,</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ReviewWeeping Icon Draws Thousands Tears Seen As Miracle...February 10, 1997 — 10 Feb 1997 — Many Greek Cypriots regard the 4...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 10, 1997</span></span></span>

Earlier reports from Cyprus also described icons apparently shedding tears, bleeding or displaying other miraculous behaviour. Such accounts were not isolated incidents but part of a wider religious culture in which sacred images were believed capable of communicating spiritual messages. Pilgrims often visited the sites hoping for healing, guidance or confirmation of faith.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.caymancompass.com/1995/03/22/cyprus-weeping-icons-bring-fame-to-tiny-chapel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: caymancompass.com">[Cayman Compass]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">caymancompass.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cyprus weeping icons bring fame to tiny chapel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cayman CompassCyprus &#x27;weeping icons&#x27; bring fame to tiny chapel22 Mar 1995 — Belief in miracles associated with icons -- weeping, bleeding...</span></span></span>

While many observers believed they had seen genuine miracles, no investigation produced evidence that persuaded the broader scientific community that a supernatural event had occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.cyprus-mail.com/2000/12/31/weeping-skies-keep-the-faithful-away-from-crying-icon/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.cyprus-mail.com">[Cyprus Mail Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.cyprus-mail.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">weeping skies keep the faithful away from crying icon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">But why they appear to come from the Virgin&#x27;s eyes no one can explain.Read more...</span></span></span>

### The Friendly Monster of Ayia Napa

On the opposite end of the spectrum lies one of Cyprus's most famous cryptid legends: the Ayia Napa sea monster. Said to inhabit waters around Cape Greco, the creature is known locally as "the Friendly Monster". Descriptions vary, but witnesses have compared it to a giant serpent, crocodile-like animal or sea dragon. Some reports claim it occasionally damaged fishing nets, though it was not considered dangerous to people.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cryptidz.fandom.com/wiki/Ayia_Napa_sea_monster" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cryptidz.fandom.com">[fandom.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cryptidz.fandom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cryptid Wiki Ayia Napa sea monster</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">There is no evidence that the monster exists except in folklore and through various...Read more...</span></span></span>

The legend became so well known that journalists and enthusiasts sometimes labelled it the "Cyprus Loch Ness Monster". Sightings were reported by fishermen, divers and tourists, yet photographs and videos remained vague, distant or impossible to verify independently. Despite decades of stories, no physical evidence has ever confirmed the existence of an unknown large marine animal in the area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cryptidz.fandom.com/wiki/Ayia_Napa_sea_monster" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cryptidz.fandom.com">[fandom.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cryptidz.fandom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cryptid Wiki Ayia Napa sea monster</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">There is no evidence that the monster exists except in folklore and through various...Read more...</span></span></span>

## How Belief, Tourism and Media Sustained the Stories

The persistence of these stories owes much to the environments in which they developed.

For miracle claims, religious tradition provides a framework through which unusual events can be interpreted. In communities where icons are venerated as sacred objects, reports of tears or fragrant oils naturally attract attention. Pilgrimage, media coverage and word-of-mouth testimony can transform a local event into a national story. Once a site gains a reputation for miracles, visitors may arrive expecting to witness something extraordinary, reinforcing the cycle of belief.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.caymancompass.com/1995/03/22/cyprus-weeping-icons-bring-fame-to-tiny-chapel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: caymancompass.com">[Cayman Compass]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">caymancompass.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cyprus weeping icons bring fame to tiny chapel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cayman CompassCyprus &#x27;weeping icons&#x27; bring fame to tiny chapel22 Mar 1995 — Belief in miracles associated with icons -- weeping, bleeding...</span></span></span>

The sea-monster story benefited from a different set of incentives. Cape Greco's dramatic coastline, sea caves and clear waters already attract visitors. A mysterious creature associated with the area adds an element of adventure and local identity. Tourism materials, travel articles and cryptid enthusiasts have helped keep the legend alive even though the evidence remains weak. The creature's harmless reputation has also made it an appealing mascot rather than a frightening menace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cyprusdiscovery.com/cyprus-caves-springs-myths/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cyprusdiscovery.com">[cyprusdiscovery.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cyprusdiscovery.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cyprus Discovery Dragons, Deep Caves, and Serpent Myths</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cyprus Discovery Dragons, Deep Caves, and Serpent Myths</span></span></span>

Media coverage often amplifies such stories because uncertainty itself is newsworthy. A confirmed miracle or monster would end the debate; an unresolved mystery keeps audiences interested. This dynamic helps explain why reports continue to circulate long after the original sightings or claims fade from memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spokesman.com/stories/1997/feb/10/weeping-icon-draws-thousands-tears-seen-as/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spokesman.com">[Spokesman-Review]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spokesman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Review Weeping Icon Draws Thousands Tears Seen As Miracle,</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ReviewWeeping Icon Draws Thousands Tears Seen As Miracle...February 10, 1997 — 10 Feb 1997 — Many Greek Cypriots regard the 4...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 10, 1997</span></span></span>

## Why Sceptics Remain Unconvinced

Sceptical explanations do not necessarily accuse witnesses of dishonesty. Instead, they focus on ordinary mechanisms that can produce extraordinary impressions.

For weeping icons, possible explanations include condensation, seepage from materials, oils within the wood or paint, environmental humidity, and other physical processes affecting old religious objects. Reports from Cyprus have noted that critics suggested the liquid originated from the icon's wooden support rather than from a supernatural source. The challenge is that many observations occurred after the phenomenon had already been reported, making independent testing difficult.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.cyprus-mail.com/2000/12/31/weeping-skies-keep-the-faithful-away-from-crying-icon/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.cyprus-mail.com">[Cyprus Mail Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.cyprus-mail.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">weeping skies keep the faithful away from crying icon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">But why they appear to come from the Virgin&#x27;s eyes no one can explain.Read more...</span></span></span>

For the Ayia Napa sea monster, sceptics point to misidentified marine animals, unusual waves, floating debris, optical distortions and the tendency of folklore to shape interpretation. A person who already knows a local monster legend may interpret an ambiguous sighting differently from someone unfamiliar with the story. The lack of clear photographs, biological samples or repeated scientific observations remains a major obstacle to accepting the creature as a real unknown species.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ancient-origins.net/myths-legends-europe/ayia-napa-sea-monster-0011785" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ancient-origins.net">[ancient-origins.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ancient-origins.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ancient OriginsThe Elusive Ayia Napa Sea Monster - Is It Really Out There?24 Apr 2019 — Although sightings of the Ayia Napa sea monster h...</span></span></span>

## Why These Stories Still Matter

The enduring appeal of Cyprus's weeping icons and sea-monster tales lies in what they reveal about uncertainty. They show how communities respond when evidence is incomplete and how narratives can flourish in the gap between observation and explanation.

Unlike clear-cut frauds, these stories do not have an obvious villain or confession. Instead, they demonstrate how belief, expectation, memory and cultural tradition can shape the interpretation of unusual events.
