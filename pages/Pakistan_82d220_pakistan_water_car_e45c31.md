## Introduction

Could [Pakistan]({{ 'how-pakistans-most-famous-hoaxes-took/' | relative_url }})’s water car ever have worked? According to the laws of physics and the evidence available from the demonstrations, the answer is no. In 2012, engineer Waqar Ahmad became a national sensation after claiming that ordinary vehicles could run on water rather than petrol or compressed natural gas. The invention arrived at a moment of fuel shortages, energy insecurity and public frustration, making the promise of a cheap domestic energy breakthrough especially attractive. Demonstrations before politicians, scientists and television audiences created the impression that a revolutionary technology had emerged. Yet once physicists examined the mechanism being described, the claim ran into a problem that no demonstration ever solved: the energy required to split water into hydrogen exceeded the useful energy that could be recovered from burning that hydrogen. The controversy became one of Pakistan’s most famous modern pseudoscientific episodes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dawn.com/news/737470/demonstration-of-water-fuelled-car-astonishes-experts" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dawn.com">[dawn.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dawn.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Demonstration of water-fuelled car astonishes experts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The engineer said the cars could be driven by a system fuelled by water instead of petrol or CNG...</span></span></span>

## What Waqar Ahmad claimed

Waqar Ahmad said he had developed a “water kit” that could be installed in conventional vehicles. According to his public explanations, the device used distilled water and produced a hydrogen-oxygen gas mixture, often referred to as HHO or oxyhydrogen, which then powered the engine. He claimed that a small car could travel roughly 40 kilometres on a litre of water and that motorcycles could travel even farther. He also suggested the technology could be adapted for electricity generation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/science/dream-come-true-a-car-that-can-run-on-water/articleshow/15221869.cms" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: timesofindia.indiatimes.com">[indiatimes.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">timesofindia.indiatimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Times of India Dream come true?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A car that can run on water28 Jul 2012 — Waqar Ahmad drove his car using water as fuel on Thursday during a demonstration for Pakistani p...</span></span></span>

The claims attracted extraordinary attention. Demonstrations were organised in Islamabad for parliamentarians, scientists, students and officials. News reports described observers as astonished by what appeared to be a vehicle operating without conventional fuel. Government figures expressed interest, and the invention was discussed as a possible answer to Pakistan’s energy difficulties.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dawn.com/news/737470/demonstration-of-water-fuelled-car-astonishes-experts" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dawn.com">[dawn.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dawn.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Demonstration of water-fuelled car astonishes experts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The engineer said the cars could be driven by a system fuelled by water instead of petrol or CNG...</span></span></span>

Importantly, Ahmad did not claim that water itself burned. Instead, he argued that his system extracted hydrogen from water and used that hydrogen as fuel. This distinction made the story sound more plausible to many observers because hydrogen is a genuine fuel used in established scientific and industrial applications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water-fuelled_car" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Water-fuelled car</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Water-fuelled car</span></span></span>

## Why the demonstration looked convincing

The demonstrations succeeded because they appeared to show a real car moving under its own power. To a non-specialist audience, that visual evidence seemed more persuasive than abstract discussions about thermodynamics. If a car is driving, many people assume the underlying engineering problem must already have been solved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dawn.com/news/737470/demonstration-of-water-fuelled-car-astonishes-experts" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dawn.com">[Dawn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dawn.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Demonstration of water-fuelled car astonishes experts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The engineer said the cars could be driven by a system fuelled by water instead of petrol or CNG...</span></span></span>

Several other factors helped the claim spread:

* **Hydrogen is a real fuel.** Cars can be powered by hydrogen under the right conditions, so the basic concept did not sound impossible.
* **Water was visibly present.** Observers could see water containers and associated equipment, reinforcing the impression that water was the key energy source.
* **Pakistan faced an energy crisis.** Rising fuel costs and electricity shortages created strong public demand for breakthrough solutions.
* **Authority figures attended demonstrations.** Appearances before politicians and scientists gave the project borrowed credibility before rigorous testing had occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dawn.com/news/737470/demonstration-of-water-fuelled-car-astonishes-experts" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dawn.com">[dawn.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dawn.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Demonstration of water-fuelled car astonishes experts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The engineer said the cars could be driven by a system fuelled by water instead of petrol or CNG...</span></span></span>

The episode illustrates a recurring pattern seen in many “water car” stories around the world. The demonstration focuses attention on a genuine process—hydrogen production—while the critical question of total energy input receives far less attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water-fuelled_car" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Water-fuelled car</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Water-fuelled car</span></span></span>

## The energy test the invention could not pass

The central issue was never whether hydrogen could be obtained from water. Scientists have been doing that for generations through electrolysis. The real question was whether the complete system produced more useful energy than it consumed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tribune.com.pk/story/417096/violating-the-laws-of-physics-on-national-television" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tribune.com.pk">[The Express Tribune]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tribune.com.pk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">violating the laws of physics on national television</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Express TribuneViolating the laws of physics on national television3 Aug 2012 — Here is what Agha Waqar is proposing: electrical ener...</span></span></span>

Ahmad’s descriptions indicated that electricity was used to split water into hydrogen and oxygen. Critics pointed out that if that electricity ultimately came from the vehicle itself—from the battery and alternator driven by the engine—then the system was trapped in a circular energy loop. Energy would be taken from the engine, used to split water, and then recovered with losses when the hydrogen was burned again.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tribune.com.pk/story/417096/violating-the-laws-of-physics-on-national-television" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tribune.com.pk">[The Express Tribune]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tribune.com.pk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">violating the laws of physics on national television</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Express TribuneViolating the laws of physics on national television3 Aug 2012 — Here is what Agha Waqar is proposing: electrical ener...</span></span></span>

This is where basic thermodynamics becomes decisive. Water is not an energy source in the way petrol is. Water is already the low-energy product created when hydrogen burns. To separate water back into hydrogen and oxygen requires an energy input. Even in an ideal system, one cannot recover more energy than was invested; in a real system, unavoidable inefficiencies mean the recovered energy is always less.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water-fuelled_car" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Water-fuelled car</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Water-fuelled car</span></span></span>

Physicists therefore argued that a proper test needed to measure every energy input and output. A convincing demonstration would have required independently verified measurements showing that the vehicle continued operating after all conventional fuel sources were removed and that the energy balance remained positive. No publicly documented test produced such evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tribune.com.pk/story/416542/the-water-car-fraud" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tribune.com.pk">[The Express Tribune]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tribune.com.pk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the water car fraud</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Express TribuneThe water car fraud2 Aug 2012 — The water car fraud Agha Waqar Ahmad&#x27;s &#x27;water kit&#x27;, if one believes science to be righ...</span></span></span>

## How scientists challenged the claim

Pakistani scientists and science commentators rapidly questioned the invention. Among the most prominent critics was physicist Pervez Hoodbhoy, who argued that the proposal contradicted fundamental thermodynamic principles accepted throughout modern engineering. His criticism was not based on a minor technical disagreement but on the claim’s conflict with the basic rules governing energy conversion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tribune.com.pk/story/416542/the-water-car-fraud" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tribune.com.pk">[The Express Tribune]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tribune.com.pk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the water car fraud</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Express TribuneThe water car fraud2 Aug 2012 — The water car fraud Agha Waqar Ahmad&#x27;s &#x27;water kit&#x27;, if one believes science to be righ...</span></span></span>

Other commentators explained the problem in practical terms. If electrolysis occurs inside a vehicle, the electricity powering that electrolysis must come from somewhere. If it comes from the vehicle’s own engine and electrical system, losses accumulate at every stage: generating electricity, splitting water, burning hydrogen and converting heat back into motion. The result is less energy than the system started with, not more.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tribune.com.pk/story/417096/violating-the-laws-of-physics-on-national-television" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tribune.com.pk">[The Express Tribune]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tribune.com.pk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">violating the laws of physics on national television</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Express TribuneViolating the laws of physics on national television3 Aug 2012 — Here is what Agha Waqar is proposing: electrical ener...</span></span></span>

As scrutiny increased, the discussion shifted away from dramatic demonstrations and toward measurable quantities. Once framed as an energy-accounting problem, the invention lost much of its apparent mystery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tribune.com.pk/story/417096/violating-the-laws-of-physics-on-national-television" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tribune.com.pk">[The Express Tribune]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tribune.com.pk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">violating the laws of physics on national television</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Express TribuneViolating the laws of physics on national television3 Aug 2012 — Here is what Agha Waqar is proposing: electrical ener...</span></span></span>

## Why the story still circulates

The water car remains memorable because it combined several powerful themes: scientific ambition, national pride, economic hardship and the dream of unlimited clean energy. Similar claims have appeared repeatedly around the world for decades, often involving secret methods of producing hydrogen or extracting energy from water.
