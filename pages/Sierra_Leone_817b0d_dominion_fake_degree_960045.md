## Introduction

In March 2022, a graduation and honorary degree ceremony held by Dominion Christian University (DCU) in Waterloo became one of [Sierra Leone]({{ 'when-false-claims-borrowed-sierra-leones/' | relative_url }})’s most discussed education scandals. The controversy was not primarily about forged certificates in the traditional sense. Instead, it centred on a deeper question: could an institution that lacked official recognition present itself as a university, award degrees and honorary doctorates, and rely on academic robes, titles and ceremonies to create the appearance of legitimacy? The answer given by Sierra Leone’s regulators was clear. The Tertiary Education Commission (TEC) stated that Dominion Christian University was not accredited to operate as a university in Sierra Leone and that its awards were not recognised. The resulting dispute exposed how easily the symbols of higher education can be mistaken for genuine academic authority.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://democracyinafrica.org/bad-education-the-politics-of-fake-degrees-in-sierra-leone/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: democracyinafrica.org">[democracyinafrica.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">democracyinafrica.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bad Education: The Politics of Fake Degrees in Sierra Leone</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 6, 2022 — 6 May 2022 — On the 26th March 2022, Dominion Christian University (DCU) located in Waterloo, Sierra Leone was seen in a vi...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 6, 2022</span></span></span>

## What Dominion Christian University Awarded

The immediate trigger for the controversy was a public graduation ceremony held on 26 March 2022 in Waterloo. During the event, Dominion Christian University awarded academic qualifications and honorary doctorate titles to a number of people. Images and videos showed recipients dressed in academic gowns and receiving certificates in a format that resembled recognised university ceremonies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://democracyinafrica.org/bad-education-the-politics-of-fake-degrees-in-sierra-leone/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: democracyinafrica.org">[democracyinafrica.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">democracyinafrica.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bad Education: The Politics of Fake Degrees in Sierra Leone</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 6, 2022 — 6 May 2022 — On the 26th March 2022, Dominion Christian University (DCU) located in Waterloo, Sierra Leone was seen in a vi...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 6, 2022</span></span></span>

The problem, according to Sierra Leone’s higher education authorities, was that the institution did not possess the accreditation required to operate as a degree-granting university. Earlier that month, the TEC had issued a public notice stating that Dominion Christian University was not accredited and directing it to cancel the planned ceremony. The commission later warned that certificates, diplomas, degrees and honorary doctorates issued by the institution were unauthorised and unrecognised within Sierra Leone’s higher education system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://democracyinafrica.org/bad-education-the-politics-of-fake-degrees-in-sierra-leone/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: democracyinafrica.org">[democracyinafrica.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">democracyinafrica.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bad Education: The Politics of Fake Degrees in Sierra Leone</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 6, 2022 — 6 May 2022 — On the 26th March 2022, Dominion Christian University (DCU) located in Waterloo, Sierra Leone was seen in a vi...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 6, 2022</span></span></span>

Dominion rejected the accusation and argued that it had been registered with local authorities and possessed international accreditation. Its representatives pointed to registrations with Sierra Leonean bodies and to overseas accrediting organisations as evidence of legitimacy. Regulators responded that business registration and recognised university accreditation are not the same thing. An organisation may legally exist as an entity while still lacking authority to award recognised academic qualifications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://democracyinafrica.org/bad-education-the-politics-of-fake-degrees-in-sierra-leone/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: democracyinafrica.org">[democracyinafrica.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">democracyinafrica.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bad Education: The Politics of Fake Degrees in Sierra Leone</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 6, 2022 — 6 May 2022 — On the 26th March 2022, Dominion Christian University (DCU) located in Waterloo, Sierra Leone was seen in a vi...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 6, 2022</span></span></span>

## Why Robes and Honorary Titles Carried Authority

The Dominion case demonstrated how much public trust is attached to the visual language of academia.

Most people do not personally inspect accreditation records before attending a graduation ceremony or seeing a doctorate awarded. Instead, they rely on familiar signals:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-stack" markdown="1">

* Academic gowns and caps.
* Formal graduation processions.
* Titles such as “Professor”, “Doctor” or “Vice-Chancellor”.
* Printed certificates carrying seals and signatures.
* Public ceremonies attended by respected figures.

</div>

These symbols exist because genuine universities use them. The difficulty is that they can also be imitated. A ceremony may look indistinguishable from a recognised university event even when the underlying institution lacks official authority to grant degrees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://democracyinafrica.org/bad-education-the-politics-of-fake-degrees-in-sierra-leone/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: democracyinafrica.org">[democracyinafrica.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">democracyinafrica.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bad Education: The Politics of Fake Degrees in Sierra Leone</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 6, 2022 — 6 May 2022 — On the 26th March 2022, Dominion Christian University (DCU) located in Waterloo, Sierra Leone was seen in a vi...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 6, 2022</span></span></span>

Honorary doctorates are especially vulnerable to misunderstanding. Legitimate universities routinely award honorary degrees to recognise achievement rather than academic study. Because many members of the public are unfamiliar with the distinction between an earned doctorate and an honorary one, honorary titles can carry considerable prestige. ## How Accreditation and Credential Checks Settle the Question

The lasting significance of the Dominion affair lies in the lesson it provided about verification.

For universities, legitimacy is not determined by robes, ceremonies, websites or promotional claims. It is determined by recognised accreditation and legal authority to grant degrees. In Sierra Leone, the relevant question was whether the institution had been approved by the national regulatory framework governing higher education. 1. **Verify accreditation with the national regulator.** Recognition must come from the authority responsible for higher education oversight, not merely from the institution itself. 3. **Examine the accrediting body.** Some institutions rely on little-known accreditation organisations whose recognition is disputed or not accepted by mainstream higher education authorities. Recognition of the accreditor matters as much as accreditation itself. [slobserver.org](https://slobserver.org/wp-content/Library/220402%20DCUSL%20-%20position%20of%20dominion%20christian%20university%20on%20TEC%20press%20release.pdf)

In this sense, the Dominion controversy became an unusually visible lesson in educational governance. The appearance of academic authority proved easier to create than genuine academic recognition.

## What Fake Degrees reveals about belief and deception

The Dominion Christian University episode remains important because it exposed a weakness that extends far beyond a single institution. Modern degree disputes rarely depend on crude forgeries. More often they involve organisations that adopt the language, ceremonies and symbols of higher education while operating outside recognised quality-control systems.

For Sierra Leone, the scandal reinforced the importance of transparent accreditation and public awareness. It showed that the public can be persuaded by the outward signs of academic legitimacy, especially when ceremonies, titles and honorary awards are involved. At the same time, it demonstrated that the most effective way to test such claims is not by examining gowns or certificates but by checking regulatory records.

As a case study in doubtful degrees, the Dominion affair illustrates a recurring pattern in the history of deception: authority is often borrowed before it is earned. The exposure came not through uncovering a hidden conspiracy but through a straightforward question asked by regulators and the public alike—was the institution actually authorised to grant the qualifications it claimed to award?
