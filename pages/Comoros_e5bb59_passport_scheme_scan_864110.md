## Introduction

The [Comoros]({{ 'when-official-stories-in-comoros-fell/' | relative_url }}) passport scandal is not a story about counterfeit documents. It is a story about genuine state-issued citizenship becoming the centre of a hidden market in nationality, with large sums of money allegedly disappearing between foreign buyers, private intermediaries and the Comorian state. What Parliament approved in 2008 as an economic citizenship programme was presented as a way to attract investment and fund development. A decade later, parliamentary investigators concluded that thousands of passports had been issued outside authorised channels, records had been manipulated, and as much as $100 million in expected revenue could not be properly accounted for.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/article/world/comoros-passport-scheme-was-unlawful-abused-by-mafia-networks-report-idUSKBN1GZ37H/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Comoros passport scheme was unlawful, abused by &quot;mafia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Comoros passport scheme was unlawful, abused by &quot;mafia...March 24, 2018 — 24 Mar 2018 — A programme to sell Comoros Islands citiz...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 24, 2018</span></span></span>

For a country with a small population and limited public resources, the allegations were extraordinary. The controversy became one of the most significant governance scandals in modern Comorian history because it combined citizenship, state authority, international politics and missing public money in a single programme. The affair also illustrates how a scheme can appear legitimate on paper while operating very differently in practice.

## What Parliament Was Told

When Comoros adopted its economic citizenship law, supporters argued that nationality could be transformed into a development asset. Foreign investors and Gulf governments would pay substantial sums in exchange for Comorian citizenship, generating funds for infrastructure and national projects. The programme was especially linked to discussions with the United Arab Emirates and Kuwait concerning stateless residents known as the Bidoon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2018/3/24/comoros-passports-abused-by-mafia-networks" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aljazeera.com">[Al Jazeera]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aljazeera.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Al Jazeera Comoros passports abused by &#x27;mafia&#x27; networks</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Al JazeeraComoros passports abused by &#x27;mafia&#x27; networksMarch 24, 2018 — 24 Mar 2018 — A programme to sell Comoros Islands citizenship resu...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 24, 2018</span></span></span>

The proposal was politically sensitive from the beginning. Critics questioned whether citizenship should be treated as a commodity at all. Supporters countered that the islands needed new sources of revenue and that the programme would bring large inflows of capital. Parliamentary debates focused on promised investment and development benefits rather than on the possibility that citizenship itself might become a highly profitable export product.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comoros_passport_sales_scandal" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Comoros passport sales scandal</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Comoros passport sales scandal</span></span></span>

The crucial point is that lawmakers were not being asked to approve a black-market operation. They were being asked to approve a state-managed economic programme. The later scandal emerged from allegations that the programme evolved far beyond what legislators believed they had authorised.

## How Official Documents Entered a Secret Market

The most striking findings of later investigations concerned the gap between authorised citizenship grants and the number of passports apparently circulating.

A parliamentary inquiry established after President Azali Assoumani returned to power examined records from the programme and concluded that thousands of passports had been issued outside approved arrangements. According to the inquiry, passport production and distribution became vulnerable to networks operating beyond normal state controls. Investigators alleged that official documents were entering channels that Parliament had never intended to create.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/article/world/comoros-passport-scheme-was-unlawful-abused-by-mafia-networks-report-idUSKBN1GZ37H/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Comoros passport scheme was unlawful, abused by &quot;mafia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Comoros passport scheme was unlawful, abused by &quot;mafia...March 24, 2018 — 24 Mar 2018 — A programme to sell Comoros Islands citiz...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 24, 2018</span></span></span>

This distinction is important. Many recipients did not possess forged passports. They possessed authentic Comorian passports. The controversy centred on whether those passports had been lawfully authorised, whether the required payments reached the treasury, and whether official records accurately reflected what had happened. In effect, the alleged deception concerned the administrative story behind the document rather than the physical document itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/article/world/comoros-passport-scheme-was-unlawful-abused-by-mafia-networks-report-idUSKBN1GZ37H/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Comoros passport scheme was unlawful, abused by &quot;mafia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Comoros passport scheme was unlawful, abused by &quot;mafia...March 24, 2018 — 24 Mar 2018 — A programme to sell Comoros Islands citiz...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 24, 2018</span></span></span>

Investigators claimed that passport issuance expanded beyond the programme's publicly stated objectives. Reports suggested that individuals with no meaningful connection to Comoros were able to obtain citizenship documents through intermediary networks and private arrangements. Some passport holders were later linked to efforts to navigate international sanctions regimes, increasing scrutiny from foreign governments and security agencies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/investigates/special-report/iran-passports-comoros/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">As sanctions bit, Iranian executives bought African passports29 Jun 2018 — More than 300 Comoros passports were sold to Iranians w...</span></span></span>

## Why the Missing Millions Mattered

The missing money was not simply an accounting problem. The entire justification for the programme rested on the promise that citizenship sales would finance national development.

According to parliamentary findings, large payments that should have benefited the state either could not be located in public accounts or appeared to have been diverted through private structures. Investigators argued that the programme had generated far less visible public benefit than expected despite the large number of passports issued.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/article/world/comoros-passport-scheme-was-unlawful-abused-by-mafia-networks-report-idUSKBN1GZ37H/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Comoros passport scheme was unlawful, abused by &quot;mafia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Comoros passport scheme was unlawful, abused by &quot;mafia...March 24, 2018 — 24 Mar 2018 — A programme to sell Comoros Islands citiz...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 24, 2018</span></span></span>

Officials involved in later investigations claimed that money paid by foreign partners did not consistently reach the treasury. The scandal therefore became a symbol of a broader governance problem: citizens were told that a national asset was being monetised for public benefit, while investigators alleged that significant portions of the proceeds were captured elsewhere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/article/world/comoros-passport-scheme-was-unlawful-abused-by-mafia-networks-report-idUSKBN1GZ37H/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Comoros passport scheme was unlawful, abused by &quot;mafia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Comoros passport scheme was unlawful, abused by &quot;mafia...March 24, 2018 — 24 Mar 2018 — A programme to sell Comoros Islands citiz...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 24, 2018</span></span></span>

For a country whose economy is small by international standards, the sums under discussion were enormous. ## Why the Story Still Circulates

The passport scandal remains one of the most discussed modern controversies in Comoros because it sits at the intersection of legality and legitimacy. The documents involved were often genuine. The programme itself was legal when created. Yet investigators alleged that the machinery surrounding it enabled a parallel market that benefited intermediaries and politically connected actors more than the state.

It is also a rare example of a national development initiative becoming a cautionary tale about citizenship as a tradable commodity. The scandal raised questions that continue to resonate far beyond Comoros: Who should be allowed to buy nationality? How can governments verify that revenues reach the public purse? And what happens when a sovereign state's most powerful document becomes valuable to people who have no real connection to the country issuing it?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/article/world/comoros-passport-scheme-was-unlawful-abused-by-mafia-networks-report-idUSKBN1GZ37H/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Comoros passport scheme was unlawful, abused by &quot;mafia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Comoros passport scheme was unlawful, abused by &quot;mafia...March 24, 2018 — 24 Mar 2018 — A programme to sell Comoros Islands citiz...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 24, 2018</span></span></span>
