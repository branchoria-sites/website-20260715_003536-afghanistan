## Introduction

The Well of Barhout, often sensationally labelled the “Well of Hell”, is one of [Yemen]({{ 'how-false-stories-took-hold-in-yemen/' | relative_url }})’s most famous examples of a real place becoming wrapped in extraordinary stories. Unlike a forged artefact or a deliberate fraud, the controversy surrounding the site centres on the boundary between folklore, fear and modern misinformation. For centuries, people in eastern Yemen associated the deep sinkhole with demons, jinn, curses and even a gateway to the underworld. When explorers finally reached the bottom in 2021, international headlines presented the descent almost as an investigation into a supernatural mystery. What they found was not evidence of hell, but a remarkable natural cave system. The story remains important because it shows how genuine landscapes can acquire reputations that spread far beyond their original folklore and become treated as factual claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well_of_Barhout" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Well of Barhout</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Well of Barhout</span></span></span>

## What People Claimed About the Pit

The Well of Barhout lies in Yemen’s eastern Al Mahra region near the border with Oman. The opening is roughly 30 metres across and descends about 112 metres into the earth. Long before it became a global internet curiosity, local traditions described it as a place best avoided.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.muscatdaily.com/2021/09/20/omani-team-explores-well-of-hell-in-yemen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muscatdaily.com">[muscatdaily.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muscatdaily.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">omani team explores well of hell in yemen</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muscat DailyOmani team explores &#x27;Well of Hell&#x27; in Yemen20 Sept 2021 — “The depth of the well is 112m, and 30m in width at the surface. De...</span></span></span>

Several recurring claims became attached to the sinkhole:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* It was supposedly created as a prison for jinn or demons.
* Some stories described it as a gateway to hell or the underworld.
* A persistent belief held that objects near the opening could be mysteriously pulled into the pit.
* Certain local traditions regarded even discussing the site as unlucky.
* The foul smell reportedly rising from the hole was interpreted by some as evidence of supernatural activity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well_of_Barhout" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Well of Barhout</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Well of Barhout</span></span></span>

</div>

These stories were not originally presented as scientific claims. They belonged to the world of regional folklore, where dramatic natural features often acquire spiritual explanations. Similar traditions exist around caves, mountains and sinkholes across many cultures. The Well of Barhout’s unusual appearance—a large circular opening in a remote desert landscape—made it especially suited to such legends.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/yemen-well-of-hell-cave-explored" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: atlasobscura.com">[Atlas Obscura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">atlasobscura.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">It&#x27;s said that visiting or even talking about it can bring bad luck.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atlas ObscuraThe History and Mystery of Yemen&#x27;s &#x27;Well of Hell&#x27;Oct 20, 2021 — For centuries, sinister legends have swirled around the Well...</span></span></span>

Problems emerged when folklore began to circulate globally through news reports, social media posts and sensational websites. Stories that had once been local traditions were increasingly presented as unresolved mysteries or possible facts. Headlines referring to a “Well of Hell” encouraged readers to treat the legends as evidence-based claims rather than cultural narratives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/yemen-well-of-hell-cave-explored" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: atlasobscura.com">[Atlas Obscura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">atlasobscura.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">It&#x27;s said that visiting or even talking about it can bring bad luck.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atlas ObscuraThe History and Mystery of Yemen&#x27;s &#x27;Well of Hell&#x27;Oct 20, 2021 — For centuries, sinister legends have swirled around the Well...</span></span></span>

## What the Cave Expedition Found

In September 2021, members of the Oman Cave Exploration Team carried out what was widely reported as the first documented descent to the bottom of the sinkhole. The expedition attracted international attention precisely because of the supernatural reputation surrounding the site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rte.ie/news/newslens/2021/0924/1248755-yemen-well-of-hell/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rte.ie">[rte.ie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rte.ie</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1248755 yemen well of hell</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Explorers venture deep into Yemen&#x27;s &#x27;Well of Hell&#x27;24 Sept 2021 — A team of Omani cavers has made what is believed to be the first descent...</span></span></span>

The findings were striking, but not supernatural.

Explorers reported:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Waterfalls flowing from openings in the cave walls.
* Stalagmites and other cave formations created by mineral deposits.
* Cave pearls formed by dripping water.
* Snakes, insects, birds, toads and other wildlife.
* Remains of dead animals and birds that had fallen into the pit.
* Geological features consistent with a natural cave and sinkhole system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well_of_Barhout" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Well of Barhout</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Well of Barhout</span></span></span>

</div>

The team also collected samples of rock, water and soil for study. Rather than discovering demons, mysterious energies or evidence of a supernatural prison, they found a natural underground environment shaped by geological processes. The unpleasant odours that had helped fuel legends could be explained by decomposing animal remains and the conditions inside the cave.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well_of_Barhout" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Well of Barhout</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Well of Barhout</span></span></span>

Importantly, the explorers did not describe the expedition as “debunking” local culture. Folklore and geology address different questions. The expedition answered whether extraordinary physical claims about the pit were supported by direct observation. They were not.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rte.ie/news/newslens/2021/0924/1248755-yemen-well-of-hell/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rte.ie">[RTE.ie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rte.ie</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1248755 yemen well of hell</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Explorers venture deep into Yemen&#x27;s &#x27;Well of Hell&#x27;24 Sept 2021 — A team of Omani cavers has made what is believed to be the first descent...</span></span></span>

## Why the Stories Seemed Plausible

The Well of Barhout illustrates how natural features can generate convincing myths without any deliberate deception.

Several factors made the legends persuasive:

**Physical isolation.** The sinkhole is located in a remote desert region. Few people had seen its interior directly, allowing speculation to flourish.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.muscatdaily.com/2021/09/20/omani-team-explores-well-of-hell-in-yemen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muscatdaily.com">[Muscat Daily]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muscatdaily.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">omani team explores well of hell in yemen</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muscat DailyOmani team explores &#x27;Well of Hell&#x27; in Yemen20 Sept 2021 — “The depth of the well is 112m, and 30m in width at the surface. De...</span></span></span>

**Dramatic appearance.** A deep circular opening descending into darkness naturally invites imagination. Many famous legends worldwide are attached to visually unusual landscapes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/yemen-well-of-hell-cave-explored" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: atlasobscura.com">[Atlas Obscura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">atlasobscura.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">It&#x27;s said that visiting or even talking about it can bring bad luck.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atlas ObscuraThe History and Mystery of Yemen&#x27;s &#x27;Well of Hell&#x27;Oct 20, 2021 — For centuries, sinister legends have swirled around the Well...</span></span></span>

**Limited exploration.** Until modern caving teams entered the site, reliable observations from the bottom were scarce. Uncertainty creates space for rumours and extraordinary explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rte.ie/news/newslens/2021/0924/1248755-yemen-well-of-hell/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rte.ie">[RTE.ie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rte.ie</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1248755 yemen well of hell</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Explorers venture deep into Yemen&#x27;s &#x27;Well of Hell&#x27;24 Sept 2021 — A team of Omani cavers has made what is believed to be the first descent...</span></span></span>

**Sensational media framing.** The nickname “Well of Hell” proved far more attractive to audiences than “large sinkhole in eastern Yemen”. News coverage often highlighted the supernatural stories before explaining the geological reality.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/yemen-well-of-hell-cave-explored" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: atlasobscura.com">[Atlas Obscura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">atlasobscura.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">It&#x27;s said that visiting or even talking about it can bring bad luck.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atlas ObscuraThe History and Mystery of Yemen&#x27;s &#x27;Well of Hell&#x27;Oct 20, 2021 — For centuries, sinister legends have swirled around the Well...</span></span></span>

The result was not a classic hoax with a known inventor, but a process in which folklore, repeated often enough, became detached from its original context and treated as an unresolved mystery.

## When Folklore Becomes Misinformation

The most interesting lesson of the Well of Barhout is that not every false belief originates with a fraudster. Many misconceptions spread because stories migrate between cultural contexts.

Within local tradition, tales about demons, bad luck or supernatural danger functioned as folklore. Outside that context, internet users and some media outlets sometimes repeated the stories as if they were factual reports.
