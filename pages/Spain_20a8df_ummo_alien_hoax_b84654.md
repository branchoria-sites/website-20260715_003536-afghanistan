## Introduction

Beginning in Madrid in the mid-1960s, the Ummo affair became one of Europe’s most elaborate UFO contact stories. What made it remarkable was not a single dramatic sighting but the creation of an entire alternative reality: thousands of pages of technical documents, anonymous telephone calls, photographs of apparent spacecraft, diagrams, symbols and detailed explanations of alien society. For decades, recipients were encouraged to believe they were communicating with emissaries from a technologically advanced civilisation called Ummo. The case is important in the history of Spanish hoaxes because it demonstrated how authority can be manufactured. Instead of demanding faith, the creators supplied paperwork, jargon, diagrams and apparent evidence until the fictional world seemed to possess its own credibility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

The Ummo story eventually became associated with José Luis Jordán Peña, a Spanish engineer and parapsychology enthusiast who later claimed responsibility for launching the deception. Yet even after his confession, the mythology survived. The endurance of the affair reveals how carefully constructed claims can outgrow their creators when enough people, documents and institutions become invested in them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ignaciodarnaude.es/ficha/2482/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ignaciodarnaude.es">[ignaciodarnaude.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ignaciodarnaude.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ignacio Darnaude Rojas-Marcos.The Ummo Experience</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Are You Experienced? ## The letters, calls and spacecraft photographs

Unlike many UFO stories that depend on a single witness account, Ummo developed through repeated contact. The earliest known communications reached circles of UFO enthusiasts centred on Madrid, particularly those linked to discussion groups around Fernando Sesma Manzano. Recipients received typed letters supposedly written by extraterrestrials living secretly on Earth after arriving from a distant planet called Ummo.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

The correspondence quickly expanded. New letters arrived from different locations and appeared to confirm one another. Anonymous phone calls added further support. The alleged visitors provided explanations of their language, social organisation, religion, science and technology. Over time, the archive grew into thousands of pages. The sheer volume itself became persuasive. Readers often assumed that no ordinary hoaxer would invest so much effort in creating such a detailed fictional universe.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

Visual evidence reinforced the narrative. Reports of UFO sightings appeared alongside photographs showing a disc-shaped craft marked with a distinctive symbol associated with Ummo. These images circulated widely among enthusiasts and were treated as confirmation that the letter writers were real. Later investigations suggested that at least some of the photographs could be reproduced using simple models suspended on fine lines and photographed under carefully controlled conditions. Researchers demonstrated how surprisingly convincing such images could appear once separated from their mundane origins.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/ummo-hoax" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[Encyclopedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">rsected with a series of UFO sightings...Read more...</span></span></span>

The result was a self-supporting system. Letters referred to sightings, sightings appeared to validate the letters, and photographs seemed to provide physical proof. Each element strengthened the others.

## Why technical language impressed readers

The most important mechanism behind Ummo was not photography but style. The letters were written in a tone that mimicked scientific and technical communication. They included diagrams, equations, specialised vocabulary and lengthy discussions of subjects ranging from physics to sociology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

This approach gave the impression that the authors possessed advanced knowledge. Readers encountered documents that looked more like research reports than spiritual revelations. Instead of promising miracles, the supposed Ummites explained how their society functioned, how their technology worked and how they viewed humanity. The format encouraged recipients to evaluate the material as information rather than mythology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

Several factors made this especially effective:

* **Quantity suggested authenticity.** The archive was so extensive that many people assumed it would be impossible for a small group to fabricate.
* **Technical complexity discouraged checking.** Most readers lacked the expertise needed to evaluate every scientific claim.
* **Selective accuracy created trust.** Genuine scientific ideas could appear alongside speculation, making the whole package seem more credible.
* **Distributed communication created independence.** Different recipients received different materials, giving the impression of a broad and coordinated extraterrestrial operation rather than a single author.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ignaciodarnaude.es/ficha/2482/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ignaciodarnaude.es">[ignaciodarnaude.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ignaciodarnaude.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ignacio Darnaude Rojas-Marcos.The Ummo Experience</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Are You Experienced? (Scott Corrales)... ORIGIN TO THE UMMO FRAUD». The article, penned by journalist and television... Jose Luis Jordan...</span></span></span>

The Ummo affair therefore anticipated techniques later seen in conspiracy theories and internet misinformation. Authority was built not through recognised institutions but through an accumulation of apparently technical evidence that few people could fully assess.

## How a fictional civilisation became an authority

One of the most striking features of Ummo is that believers often treated the alleged aliens as experts rather than visitors. The letters offered explanations for scientific, philosophical and social questions. Some recipients regarded the documents as a source of superior knowledge and debated them much as scholars debate texts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

This transformed the role of evidence. Instead of asking whether the Ummites existed, many participants discussed what the Ummites thought. The fictional civilisation acquired intellectual authority before its existence had been established. Once that happened, new documents were no longer judged solely on whether they proved alien contact. They were evaluated as contributions to an already accepted body of knowledge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ignaciodarnaude.es/ficha/2482/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ignaciodarnaude.es">[Ignacio Darnaude Rojas-Marcos.]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ignaciodarnaude.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ignacio Darnaude Rojas-Marcos.The Ummo Experience</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Are You Experienced? (Scott Corrales)... ORIGIN TO THE UMMO FRAUD». The article, penned by journalist and television... Jose Luis Jordan...</span></span></span>

The process resembles the creation of an invented tradition or a pseudoscientific literature. Repetition generated familiarity, familiarity generated trust and trust generated authority. By the 1970s and 1980s, Ummo had become far more than a collection of strange letters. It was a social network of correspondents, researchers and enthusiasts spread across [Spain]({{ 'how-spains-most-famous-hoaxes-took-hold/' | relative_url }}), France and beyond.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

## Confession, investigation and replication

Suspicion surrounded the affair for many years, but a new generation of investigators began examining the evidence more critically. Researchers noticed recurring links between major Ummo events and José Luis Jordán Peña, a figure active in Spanish parapsychological circles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ignaciodarnaude.es/ficha/2482/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ignaciodarnaude.es">[Ignacio Darnaude Rojas-Marcos.]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ignaciodarnaude.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ignacio Darnaude Rojas-Marcos.The Ummo Experience</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Are You Experienced? (Scott Corrales)... ORIGIN TO THE UMMO FRAUD». The article, penned by journalist and television... Jose Luis Jordan...</span></span></span>

In the early 1990s, Jordán Peña publicly claimed that he had initiated the Ummo operation decades earlier. He described it as a social experiment intended to explore belief and suggestion. His account included explanations of how letters were produced and how photographs could be staged. According to his version, the project had expanded far beyond its original scope and attracted imitators who contributed their own material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ignaciodarnaude.es/ficha/2482/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ignaciodarnaude.es">[ignaciodarnaude.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ignaciodarnaude.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ignacio Darnaude Rojas-Marcos.The Ummo Experience</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Are You Experienced? (Scott Corrales)... ORIGIN TO THE UMMO FRAUD». The article, penned by journalist and television... Jose Luis Jordan...</span></span></span>

Investigators strengthened the sceptical case by recreating some of the famous photographs using simple physical models. The success of these reproductions undermined the claim that the images required genuine extraterrestrial craft. Yet the confession did not settle every argument. Some believers pointed to inconsistencies in Jordán Peña’s statements and questioned whether a single individual could have generated such a vast archive over so many years.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

That disagreement itself became part of the mythology. The confession was treated by sceptics as the solution to the mystery and by some believers as merely another layer of the puzzle.

## Why the hoax survived its creator

Most hoaxes collapse when their mechanism is exposed. Ummo followed a different path. The story survived because its authority no longer depended entirely on its original source. By the time the confession arrived, thousands of documents had circulated, numerous people had contributed to the narrative and decades of discussion had created a community invested in the mystery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

The affair also benefited from a familiar psychological pattern. People who had spent years studying the documents often found it easier to doubt the confession than to abandon the framework they had built around the material. Some argued that Jordán Peña had only written part of the archive. Others suggested intelligence agencies, additional collaborators or genuine unknown influences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ignaciodarnaude.es/ficha/2482/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ignaciodarnaude.es">[ignaciodarnaude.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ignaciodarnaude.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ignacio Darnaude Rojas-Marcos.The Ummo Experience</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Are You Experienced? ## What Ummo reveals about belief and evidence

The enduring significance of Ummo lies in its method. It showed that a persuasive hoax does not necessarily need forged artefacts of great age or dramatic eyewitness testimony.
