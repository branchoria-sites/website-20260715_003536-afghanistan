## Introduction

In early 2025, police raids in Koror revealed that some ordinary-looking hotels in [Palau]({{ 'how-deception-borrowed-trust-in-palau/' | relative_url }})'s commercial centre were allegedly being used as bases for sophisticated online fraud operations. Investigators found rooms packed with computers, foreign workers and communications equipment rather than tourists. According to reporting based on leaked official documents and law-enforcement records, the operations were linked to the same broad family of scams that have spread across parts of Southeast Asia: fake online relationships, bogus cryptocurrency investments, illegal gambling platforms and money-laundering networks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.occrp.org/en/investigation/foreign-workers-local-sponsors-inside-palaus-hotel-scam-centers" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: occrp.org">[OCCRP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">occrp.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">foreign workers local sponsors inside palaus hotel scam centers</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Foreign Workers, Local Sponsors: Inside Palau&#x27;s Hotel...22 Dec 2025 — A trove of documents unveils the inner workings of two appare...</span></span></span>

## How the Koror Operations Were Organised and Discovered

The best-documented cases emerged after Palauan authorities raided hotels in Koror during January 2025. Investigators reportedly encountered conditions resembling scam compounds previously exposed in Cambodia, Myanmar and the Philippines: large numbers of foreign workers operating computers in tightly controlled environments. Some workers later told authorities they had spent years inside the buildings with limited freedom of movement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.operationshamrock.org/library/2025-12-occrp-palau" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: operationshamrock.org">[operationshamrock.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">operationshamrock.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2025 12 occrp palau</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inside Palau&#x27;s Hotel Scam Centers22 Dec 2025 — When police raided hotel sites in Palau, they encountered scenes that looked much like tho...</span></span></span>

## How False Profiles and Investment Dashboards Manipulated Victims

The fraud techniques associated with the Koror operations closely resembled what investigators elsewhere call "pig-butchering" scams. The name refers to a process in which victims are cultivated gradually before being persuaded to make increasingly large financial transfers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scam_centers_in_Cambodia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wikipedia.org">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wikipedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Scam centers in Cambodia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Scam centers in Cambodia</span></span></span>

The process generally followed several stages:

1. **Initial contact** through social media, messaging apps, dating platforms or seemingly accidental messages.
2. **Relationship building**, sometimes lasting weeks or months.
3. **Introduction of an investment opportunity**, often involving cryptocurrency trading.
4. **Use of fake dashboards** showing impressive profits and successful trades.
5. **Escalating deposits** as victims became convinced the investment was genuine.

6. **Withdrawal barriers**, with victims told to pay additional taxes, fees or verification charges before accessing their money. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scam_centers_in_Cambodia)

The crucial deception was not merely the fake investment platform. It was the false identity behind the screen. Victims believed they were interacting with a successful investor, a romantic interest or a trusted friend. In reality, communications were often scripted and monitored by organised teams. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scam_centers_in_Cambodia)

Leaked records cited by investigators suggested that one Palau-based operation generated substantial monthly revenues, with proceeds allegedly moved offshore through cryptocurrency channels. The use of digital assets complicated efforts to trace funds and recover losses once victims realised they had been deceived. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/bernadette-carreon-19530678_foreign-workers-local-sponsors-inside-palaus-activity-7408925779264229378-2ffh)

## Why Workers Could Be Both Participants and Trafficking Victims

One of the most important lessons from the Koror cases is that the people sending fraudulent messages were not always willing participants.

International organisations and law-enforcement agencies increasingly describe modern scam centres as a hybrid of cybercrime and human trafficking. Workers are frequently recruited through promises of legitimate jobs, only to discover that they are expected to conduct online fraud. Passports may be confiscated, movement restricted and punishment imposed for failing to meet targets. [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/en/press-releases/2026/02/un-report-details-grave-abuses-against-those-trafficked-scam-centres)

Accounts connected to the Palau investigations contained similar allegations. Some workers reportedly described long periods of confinement inside hotel facilities and limited ability to leave. These claims echoed patterns documented throughout the broader scam-centre industry. [operationshamrock.org](https://www.operationshamrock.org/library/2025-12-occrp-palau)

This creates a moral complexity often lost in public discussion. The person operating a fake profile may be committing fraud against victims abroad while simultaneously experiencing coercion or exploitation. Understanding that dual reality has become central to modern investigations of online scam networks. [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/en/press-releases/2026/02/un-report-details-grave-abuses-against-those-trafficked-scam-centres)

## Why Palau Became Attractive to Scam Networks

The Koror operations did not emerge in isolation. Investigators and journalists have argued that Palau's small size, limited enforcement resources and legal gaps created opportunities for criminal organisers seeking new locations as authorities increased pressure elsewhere in Asia. [OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/investigation/foreign-workers-local-sponsors-inside-palaus-hotel-scam-centers)

Palau's tourism infrastructure also offered practical advantages. Hotels already contained rooms, internet connections and accommodation space for workers. A fraud operation could therefore be hidden behind a business that appeared ordinary from the outside. [OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/investigation/foreign-workers-local-sponsors-inside-palaus-hotel-scam-centers)

International policing organisations have warned that scam-centre networks are highly mobile. When enforcement intensifies in one country, organisers often relocate personnel, equipment and financial infrastructure to another jurisdiction. The appearance of hotel-based centres in Koror fits this broader pattern of geographical adaptation. [Interpol+2Reuters](https://www.interpol.int/en/News-and-Events/News/2025/INTERPOL-releases-new-information-on-globalization-of-scam-centres)

## What the Koror Cases Reveal About Modern Deception

Unlike classic hoaxes built around forged objects or fabricated stories, the Koror scam-centre cases demonstrate a distinctly digital form of deception. The fraud depended on creating believable online personas, convincing victims that fabricated profits were real and exploiting the emotional trust that develops through repeated communication. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scam_centers_in_Cambodia)

The exposure of these operations also shows how modern fraud frequently combines several layers of deception at once:

* False personal identities.
* Fake romantic relationships.
* Fabricated investment platforms.
* Manipulated account balances and profit reports.
* Hidden ownership structures and cryptocurrency transfers. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scam_centers_in_Cambodia)

Within Palau's wider history of contested truth, the Koror hotel scam centres stand out because they transformed everyday commercial spaces into factories for manufactured trust. The hotel rooms themselves were real. The computers were real. The conversations were real. What was false were the identities, relationships and investment opportunities presented to victims around the world. [OCCRP+2operationshamrock.org](https://www.occrp.org/en/investigation/foreign-workers-local-sponsors-inside-palaus-hotel-scam-centers)
