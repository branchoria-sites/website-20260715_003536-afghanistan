## Introduction

The Kanakaria mosaics case is not famous because the artworks were forged. The mosaics themselves were genuine sixth-century masterpieces. The deception lay elsewhere: in the attempt to give looted cultural property a respectable ownership history so that it could be sold through legitimate art-market channels. In that sense, the case became one of the clearest demonstrations of how provenance laundering works. A stolen object is not transformed physically; instead, its history is rewritten until buyers can persuade themselves that it entered the market legitimately.

For [Cyprus]({{ 'when-cyprus-stories-became-more/' | relative_url }}), the dispute became a landmark battle over cultural heritage after religious artworks were removed from churches in the island’s north following the upheavals of the 1970s. For the international art trade, it became a warning that an attractive ownership story is not the same thing as documented ownership. Courts ultimately concluded that the mosaics had been trafficked through a chain of concealment and that buyers had ignored warning signs that should have prompted deeper investigation. The resulting legal decisions helped reshape expectations about due diligence in the antiquities market.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/case-law-doc/traffickingculturalpropertycrimetype/usa/1990/case_kanakaria_mosaics_html/Autocephalous_Church_v._Goldberg_Feldman_Arts_917_F.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unodc.org">[unodc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unodc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Autocephalous Church v</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Goldberg &amp; Feldman Arts, 917 F....by SCJ PELL —... court&#x27;s decision to award the possession of the mosaics to the Church of Cyprus...</span></span></span>

## The Removal and Sale of the Kanakaria Mosaics

The mosaics came from the apse of the Church of Panagia Kanakaria at Lythrangomi in northern Cyprus. Created around the sixth century, they were among the most important surviving examples of early Byzantine mosaic art and had survived periods of iconoclasm that destroyed many comparable works elsewhere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.arthurbrand.com/track-record/cyprus-mosaic-st-mark/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arthurbrand.com">[Arthur Brand]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arthurbrand.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cyprus mosaic st mark</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Arthur BrandCyprus the mosaic of St Mark21 Aug 2018 — The church of Panaya Kanakaria dates back to the early Byzantine period (5th centur...</span></span></span>

Sometime after the division of Cyprus, the mosaics were cut from the church wall, broken into transportable sections and moved into the international black market. Their removal caused severe damage. What had once been part of a fixed architectural setting became a collection of detached fragments that could be traded individually.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/lifestyle/cyprus-museum-showcases-rare-religious-treasures-recovered-after-looting-2025-03-20/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Many of these pieces, including mosaics, icons, and wall frescoes, were retrieved after being looted from northern churches following the...</span></span></span>

The crucial stage in the story was not the theft itself but the effort to sell the pieces. Art dealer Peg Goldberg acquired several mosaic fragments in 1988 through intermediaries linked to the Turkish art dealer Aydin Dikmen. The fragments were then offered to major institutions and collectors. According to accounts presented during litigation, explanations circulated that the mosaics had come from an abandoned church or had otherwise entered private hands legitimately. These narratives sought to make the objects appear marketable despite the absence of a reliable ownership trail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/case-law-doc/traffickingculturalpropertycrimetype/usa/1990/case_kanakaria_mosaics_html/Autocephalous_Church_v._Goldberg_Feldman_Arts_917_F.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unodc.org">[unodc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unodc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Autocephalous Church v</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Goldberg &amp; Feldman Arts, 917 F....by SCJ PELL —... court&#x27;s decision to award the possession of the mosaics to the Church of Cyprus...</span></span></span>

The strategy reflected a common pattern in illicit antiquities trafficking. Stolen objects are often difficult to sell immediately because their origins are too obvious. Creating a plausible ownership history can increase their apparent legitimacy and dramatically raise their value. The Kanakaria case became a textbook example because the provenance story collapsed under scrutiny.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://insight.dickinsonlaw.psu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1126&amp;context=psilr" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: insight.dickinsonlaw.psu.edu">[Insight]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">insight.dickinsonlaw.psu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Insight Autocephalous Greek Orthodox Church of Cyprus v</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">M Van Pelt · 1990 · Cited by 16 — The Byzantine mosaics purchased by Peg Goldberg, an Indiana art... tries where a choice...</span></span></span>

## Warning Signs in the Ownership Story

One reason the case remains influential is that the warning signs were unusually clear.

The mosaics were internationally recognised works rather than obscure archaeological fragments. They came from a known church and had been documented before their disappearance. Their sudden appearance on the market therefore demanded an explanation supported by evidence rather than informal assurances.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/lifestyle/cyprus-museum-showcases-rare-religious-treasures-recovered-after-looting-2025-03-20/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Many of these pieces, including mosaics, icons, and wall frescoes, were retrieved after being looted from northern churches following the...</span></span></span>

Several factors raised concerns:

* The ownership chain was incomplete and poorly documented.
* The artworks had emerged from a region affected by conflict and widespread cultural-property theft.
* The transaction relied heavily on verbal explanations rather than a verifiable paper trail.
* The mosaics were being offered at a time when their disappearance from Cyprus was already known among specialists.
* The physical condition of the fragments reflected violent removal from an architectural setting rather than a long-established collecting history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/case-law-doc/traffickingculturalpropertycrimetype/usa/1990/case_kanakaria_mosaics_html/Autocephalous_Church_v._Goldberg_Feldman_Arts_917_F.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unodc.org">[unodc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unodc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Autocephalous Church v</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Goldberg &amp; Feldman Arts, 917 F....by SCJ PELL —... court&#x27;s decision to award the possession of the mosaics to the Church of Cyprus...</span></span></span>

The case is often discussed as an example of how provenance laundering exploits ambiguity. A buyer may not be presented with an obviously false story. Instead, gaps are filled with claims that sound possible but cannot be verified. The resulting uncertainty can create enough apparent legitimacy for a transaction to proceed.

The Getty Museum played an important indirect role in the exposure of the scheme. When the mosaics were shown to potential institutional buyers, experts recognised them as the missing Kanakaria works. That recognition helped connect the fragments to the church from which they had been removed and undermined attempts to treat them as objects of uncertain origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Looted_art" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Looted art</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Looted art</span></span></span>

## The Court Case That Changed Due Diligence

The legal battle that followed became one of the most important cultural-property cases of the late twentieth century. The Autocephalous Greek Orthodox Church of Cyprus and the Republic of Cyprus sued for the return of the mosaics in the United States. Federal courts ultimately ruled in their favour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/case-law-doc/traffickingculturalpropertycrimetype/usa/1990/case_kanakaria_mosaics_html/Autocephalous_Church_v._Goldberg_Feldman_Arts_917_F.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unodc.org">[UNODC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unodc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Autocephalous Church v</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Goldberg &amp; Feldman Arts, 917 F....by SCJ PELL —... court&#x27;s decision to award the possession of the mosaics to the Church of Cyprus...</span></span></span>

The courts rejected the argument that good-faith purchase could overcome the rights of the original owners. Judges concluded that the mosaics had been stolen and that ownership could not be transferred through illicit transactions. Equally important, the courts examined whether the buyer had exercised sufficient caution before purchasing such exceptional objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/case-law-doc/traffickingculturalpropertycrimetype/usa/1990/case_kanakaria_mosaics_html/Autocephalous_Church_v._Goldberg_Feldman_Arts_917_F.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unodc.org">[unodc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unodc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Autocephalous Church v</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Goldberg &amp; Feldman Arts, 917 F....by SCJ PELL —... court&#x27;s decision to award the possession of the mosaics to the Church of Cyprus...</span></span></span>

The rulings emphasised that purchasers of high-value antiquities cannot simply accept convenient stories at face value. Where circumstances suggest possible theft or illegal export, buyers are expected to investigate. The decisions also addressed issues of concealment and discovery, recognising that trafficked cultural objects are often deliberately hidden in ways that make recovery difficult for the original owners.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://plone.unige.ch/art-adr/cases-affaires/kanakaria-mosaics-2013-autocephalous-greek-orthodox-church-of-cyprus-and-cyprus-v-goldberg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: plone.unige.ch">[unige.ch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">plone.unige.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">and discover the fraud which is a requirement to invoke the doctrine of fraudulent...Read more...</span></span></span>
