## Introduction

Sanctuary [Belize]({{ 'how-belize-became-a-stage-for-deception/' | relative_url }}) was not a fake place. Buyers really did acquire interests in real land on Belize’s Caribbean coast. The deception, according to United States regulators and later court findings, lay in the promises surrounding that land: claims about financing, construction schedules, luxury amenities, resale opportunities and investment returns that helped transform an unfinished development into a highly successful sales operation. Over more than a decade, promoters marketed Sanctuary Belize as a future high-end resort community where property values would soar as hospitals, marinas, golf courses, airports and other facilities appeared. Regulators later concluded that many of those claims were false or misleading and that the project became one of the largest overseas real-estate fraud cases ever pursued by the US Federal Trade Commission (FTC).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2018/11/ftcs-request-court-halts-massive-sanctuary-belize-real-estate-investment-scam" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ftc.gov">[Federal Trade Commission]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ftc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Trade Commission At FTC&#x27;s Request, Court Halts Massive “Sanctuary Belize</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Trade CommissionAt FTC&#x27;s Request, Court Halts Massive “Sanctuary Belize...November 7, 2018 — 8 Nov 2018 — According to the FTC...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2018</span></span></span>

For a history of deception in Belize, the case is notable because it did not depend on forged documents or imaginary property. Instead, it used a genuine development project as the foundation for a larger fiction about risk, progress and future value. Buyers were sold a vision of paradise that appeared credible precisely because some real construction existed and because the land itself was real.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/x040009-re-sanctuary-belize-litigation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ftc.gov">[Federal Trade Commission]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ftc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Trade Commission In re Sanctuary Belize Litigation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Trade CommissionIn re Sanctuary Belize LitigationNovember 7, 2018 — 8 Nov 2018 — The FTC alleged that the defendants misled consu...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2018</span></span></span>

## The Luxury Resort Promises Made to Overseas Buyers

Sanctuary Belize was marketed primarily to American consumers, many of them approaching retirement or looking for overseas investment opportunities. Promotional campaigns appeared through television advertising, infomercials, webinars, telemarketing calls and organised visits to Belize. The development was also promoted under related names including Sanctuary Bay and The Reserve.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2018/11/ftcs-request-court-halts-massive-sanctuary-belize-real-estate-investment-scam" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ftc.gov">[ftc.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ftc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Trade Commission At FTC&#x27;s Request, Court Halts Massive “Sanctuary Belize</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Trade CommissionAt FTC&#x27;s Request, Court Halts Massive “Sanctuary Belize...November 7, 2018 — 8 Nov 2018 — According to the FTC...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2018</span></span></span>

According to the FTC and later court findings, the sales pitch revolved around several recurring claims:

* The project supposedly operated under a “no-debt” model that made it safer than conventional developments.
* Money from lot sales was said to be reinvested directly into construction and infrastructure.
* Major facilities would be completed within a relatively short period, often presented as only a few years away.
* The community would include luxury amenities such as a marina village, golf course, hospital, airport and other high-end facilities.
* Property values were expected to rise dramatically as development progressed.
* Owners would be able to resell their lots easily in a strong secondary market.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2018/11/ftcs-request-court-halts-massive-sanctuary-belize-real-estate-investment-scam" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ftc.gov">[ftc.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ftc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Trade Commission At FTC&#x27;s Request, Court Halts Massive “Sanctuary Belize</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Trade CommissionAt FTC&#x27;s Request, Court Halts Massive “Sanctuary Belize...November 7, 2018 — 8 Nov 2018 — According to the FTC...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2018</span></span></span>

These promises were persuasive because they combined two powerful appeals. One was lifestyle: retirement in a tropical English-speaking country with modern facilities. The other was investment: buyers were encouraged to believe they were purchasing property before a major boom in value. Accounts from investors and regulatory filings show that some purchasers committed large portions of retirement savings in anticipation of both outcomes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://therealdeal.com/miami/2018/11/23/caribbean-real-estate-dream-turns-into-100-million-nightmare-for-retirees/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: therealdeal.com">[The Real Deal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">therealdeal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Real DealCaribbean real estate dream turns into $100 million...23 Nov 2018 — Promoters allegedly fleeced 1,000-plus lot buyers out o...</span></span></span>

## How Genuine Land Supported a Larger Financial Fiction

Unlike many property scams, Sanctuary Belize did not revolve around non-existent plots. The land existed, and some infrastructure and residential construction had been completed. That reality gave the project a degree of credibility that purely fictional schemes lack. Buyers could visit the site, see development activity and receive legal documentation connected to actual parcels.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca4/20-2215/20-2215-2022-11-01.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.justia.com">[Justia Law]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.justia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Justia LawFederal Trade Commission v. The FTC’s case was therefore not that the land itself was fake. Instead, regulators argued that the surrounding narrative was deceptive. Court records and regulatory filings describe representations about funding, risk and construction progress that allegedly concealed the true state of the project. The “debt-free” claim became especially important because it was presented as proof that purchasers faced less risk than investors in traditional developments. Evidence presented in court indicated that these assurances were misleading and that the project carried debts despite repeated claims to the contrary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sanctuarybelizereceivership.com/documents/211" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sanctuarybelizereceivership.com">[sanctuarybelizereceivership.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sanctuarybelizereceivership.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sanctuarybelizereceivership.com.</span></span></span>

Another crucial element was the promise that every dollar from lot sales would be reinvested in development. Buyers were encouraged to view new sales as a self-sustaining engine that would rapidly create the luxury community being advertised. Regulators later argued that this picture bore little resemblance to reality and that the project could not deliver the amenities and timelines being promised.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2018/11/ftcs-request-court-halts-massive-sanctuary-belize-real-estate-investment-scam" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ftc.gov">[Federal Trade Commission]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ftc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Trade Commission At FTC&#x27;s Request, Court Halts Massive “Sanctuary Belize</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Trade CommissionAt FTC&#x27;s Request, Court Halts Massive “Sanctuary Belize...November 7, 2018 — 8 Nov 2018 — According to the FTC...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2018</span></span></span>

This distinction explains why the case occupies an unusual place in discussions of fraud. The development was neither wholly imaginary nor wholly legitimate. Real land ownership helped support confidence in claims about future infrastructure, future demand and future value that regulators said were materially misleading.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca4/20-2215/20-2215-2022-11-01.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.justia.com">[Justia Law]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.justia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Justia LawFederal Trade Commission v. ## Why Buyers Found the Story Convincing

The Sanctuary Belize sales campaign emerged during a period when overseas property investment was widely promoted to North American retirees. Belize possessed several advantages as a marketing destination: English is the official language, the country sits on the Caribbean coast, and foreign buyers were often attracted by perceptions of lower costs and a relaxed lifestyle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://therealdeal.com/miami/2018/11/23/caribbean-real-estate-dream-turns-into-100-million-nightmare-for-retirees/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: therealdeal.com">[The Real Deal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">therealdeal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Real DealCaribbean real estate dream turns into $100 million...23 Nov 2018 — Promoters allegedly fleeced 1,000-plus lot buyers out o...</span></span></span>

The promoters also used a common feature of speculative real-estate marketing: scarcity combined with projected future growth. Buyers were told they were entering before major amenities arrived and before prices rose dramatically. The development’s promotional material presented unfinished infrastructure as evidence of progress rather than as a warning that completion remained uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2018/11/ftcs-request-court-halts-massive-sanctuary-belize-real-estate-investment-scam" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ftc.gov">[Federal Trade Commission]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ftc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Trade Commission At FTC&#x27;s Request, Court Halts Massive “Sanctuary Belize</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Trade CommissionAt FTC&#x27;s Request, Court Halts Massive “Sanctuary Belize...November 7, 2018 — 8 Nov 2018 — According to the FTC...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2018</span></span></span>

Because some roads, buildings and facilities did exist, prospective purchasers could interpret visible construction as confirmation that the broader vision was on schedule. That made the more ambitious promises about airports, hospitals and rapid appreciation appear plausible even when they were not supported by completed projects on the ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2018/11/ftcs-request-court-halts-massive-sanctuary-belize-real-estate-investment-scam" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ftc.gov">[Federal Trade Commission]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ftc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Trade Commission At FTC&#x27;s Request, Court Halts Massive “Sanctuary Belize</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Trade CommissionAt FTC&#x27;s Request, Court Halts Massive “Sanctuary Belize...November 7, 2018 — 8 Nov 2018 — According to the FTC...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2018</span></span></span>

## The FTC Case and the Consequences for Investors

In 2018, the FTC filed a major enforcement action alleging that Sanctuary Belize promoters had operated a deceptive international real-estate investment scheme. The agency described it as the largest overseas real-estate investment scam it had ever targeted and alleged that more than $100 million had been collected from consumers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ftc.gov/media/71286" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ftc.gov">[Federal Trade Commission]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ftc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Trade CommissionCourt Halts Massive “Sanctuary Belize” Real Estate...Serial scammer Andris Pukke and others are charged with dec...</span></span></span>

## What the Case Reveals About Modern Property Fraud

Sanctuary Belize demonstrates that deception does not always require fabricated assets. A genuine development can become the centre of a misleading narrative when promoters exaggerate funding, timelines, amenities or future returns. The existence of real land may actually make the story more convincing because buyers can see something tangible behind the sales pitch.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/x040009-re-sanctuary-belize-litigation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ftc.gov">[Federal Trade Commission]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ftc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Trade Commission In re Sanctuary Belize Litigation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Trade CommissionIn re Sanctuary Belize LitigationNovember 7, 2018 — 8 Nov 2018 — The FTC alleged that the defendants misled consu...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2018</span></span></span>
