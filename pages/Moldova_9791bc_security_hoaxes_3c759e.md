## Introduction

After Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, [Moldova]({{ 'how-false-stories-took-hold-in-moldova/' | relative_url }}) became unusually vulnerable to security rumours, bomb hoaxes and false wartime alerts. The country borders Ukraine, hosts a breakaway region with Russian troops present in Transnistria, and has long been exposed to competing information campaigns from East and West. In that environment, even implausible claims could appear believable if they echoed genuine fears about war, mobilisation or political instability.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/fake-bombs-failed-coup-moldova-smolders-border-russias-war-2023-03-10/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fake bombs and failed coup: Moldova smolders on border</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fake bombs and failed coup: Moldova smolders on border...March 10, 2023 — 14 Mar 2023 — A coup attempt, bomb hoaxes, internet hac...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 10, 2023</span></span></span>

Unlike classic hoaxes built around forged artefacts or invented legends, Moldova’s security scares were often designed to create disruption rather than persuade people of a single false story. Anonymous bomb threats forced evacuations, fake mobilisation notices circulated online, and rumours of invasion or coups spread rapidly through social media and messaging apps. The result was a climate in which citizens repeatedly had to distinguish between real security concerns and manufactured panic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/fake-bombs-failed-coup-moldova-smolders-border-russias-war-2023-03-10/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fake bombs and failed coup: Moldova smolders on border</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fake bombs and failed coup: Moldova smolders on border...March 10, 2023 — 14 Mar 2023 — A coup attempt, bomb hoaxes, internet hac...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 10, 2023</span></span></span>

## Why Moldova Became a Target for Security Scares

Moldova’s position next to a major war made it particularly susceptible to rumours. The country was already dealing with refugee flows, economic pressure, concerns about Transnistria and occasional incidents linked to the conflict in neighbouring Ukraine. Missile debris from attacks on Ukraine was found several times on Moldovan territory, reminding the public that the war was not entirely distant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/8d35f4ea80376c6e9828e79c7f195e22" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AP News Rocket debris found again in Moldova, from war next door</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The wreckage was found in Larga, Briceni county, near the Ukrainian border, prompting the deployment of a bomb squad and the area being c...</span></span></span>

This mattered because effective rumours rarely emerge from nothing. They usually attach themselves to real events. When people had already seen airspace closures, missile debris investigations or heightened security alerts, stories about secret mobilisation, imminent invasion or sabotage appeared more plausible than they would have in calmer circumstances.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Moldovan_coup_attempt_allegations" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2023 Moldovan coup attempt allegations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">2023 Moldovan coup attempt allegations</span></span></span>

Researchers studying information warfare around Moldova have argued that fear itself became a target. Messages did not always need to convince citizens that a specific claim was true. Simply encouraging uncertainty about whether war might spread into Moldova could undermine confidence in institutions and increase public anxiety.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sceeus.se/publikationer/russias-hybrid-war-against-moldova/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sceeus.se">[Sceeus]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sceeus.se</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Russia&#x27;s Hybrid War Against MoldovaThe threat of war – especially against the backdrop of Russia&#x27;s aggression in Ukraine – creates...</span></span></span>

## Waves of Bomb Threats and False Alarms

One of the most visible forms of disruption was the wave of bomb threats that struck Moldova after the invasion of Ukraine began. Airports, courts, government buildings and other public facilities received warnings that later proved false. The threats forced evacuations, consumed police resources and generated extensive media coverage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2022/7/29/a-summer-of-bomb-threats-frightens-moldova-as-war-rages-nearby" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aljazeera.com">[Al Jazeera]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aljazeera.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">a summer of bomb threats frightens moldova as war rages nearby</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Al JazeeraA summer of bomb threats frightens Moldova as war rages...29 Jul 2022 — Moldovans fear a Russian invasion as tensions between...</span></span></span>

By mid-2022, the volume of threats had become large enough to be treated as a national security issue. Public buildings were repeatedly searched, transport was disrupted and ordinary activities were interrupted by emergency responses. Although the threats generally failed to uncover actual explosive devices, the disruption itself achieved the apparent objective of creating tension and uncertainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2022/7/29/a-summer-of-bomb-threats-frightens-moldova-as-war-rages-nearby" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aljazeera.com">[Al Jazeera]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aljazeera.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">a summer of bomb threats frightens moldova as war rages nearby</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Al JazeeraA summer of bomb threats frightens Moldova as war rages...29 Jul 2022 — Moldovans fear a Russian invasion as tensions between...</span></span></span>

Reuters later described bomb hoaxes as one element in a broader pattern of pressure facing Moldova during the war period, alongside cyberattacks, disinformation campaigns and alleged destabilisation efforts. Moldovan officials argued that the accumulation of incidents mattered as much as any individual threat because repeated alarms could erode public confidence and create a sense of permanent crisis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/fake-bombs-failed-coup-moldova-smolders-border-russias-war-2023-03-10/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fake bombs and failed coup: Moldova smolders on border</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fake bombs and failed coup: Moldova smolders on border...March 10, 2023 — 14 Mar 2023 — A coup attempt, bomb hoaxes, internet hac...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 10, 2023</span></span></span>

The phenomenon did not disappear after the initial shock of 2022. Bomb alerts continued to appear in politically sensitive contexts, demonstrating how anonymous threats could be used to disrupt normal public life even when few people believed a real bomb was present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/russia-issues-protest-moldova-over-embassy-bomb-alert-2025-03-19/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Russia issues protest to Moldova over embassy bomb alert</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Russia issues protest to Moldova over embassy bomb alert</span></span></span>

## Fake Mobilisation and Invasion Claims

Alongside bomb threats came rumours that Moldova was preparing for war. False mobilisation notices circulated online claiming that citizens would soon be called into military service. Other stories alleged secret preparations for direct participation in the conflict or claimed that Moldova was on the verge of being drawn into combat.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/fake-bombs-failed-coup-moldova-smolders-border-russias-war-2023-03-10/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fake bombs and failed coup: Moldova smolders on border</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fake bombs and failed coup: Moldova smolders on border...March 10, 2023 — 14 Mar 2023 — A coup attempt, bomb hoaxes, internet hac...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 10, 2023</span></span></span>

Such claims exploited a genuine concern. Moldova has a small military, sits beside an active war zone and contains the unresolved issue of Transnistria. Because the strategic environment was uncertain, fabricated documents and unofficial announcements could spread before authorities had time to respond.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sceeus.se/publikationer/russias-hybrid-war-against-moldova/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sceeus.se">[Sceeus]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sceeus.se</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Russia&#x27;s Hybrid War Against MoldovaThe threat of war – especially against the backdrop of Russia&#x27;s aggression in Ukraine – creates...</span></span></span>

Rumours also appeared around supposed invasion scenarios. Some stories claimed Russian forces would imminently enter Moldova, while others alleged secret plans involving neighbouring states or foreign troops. These narratives often recycled fragments of authentic news, such as military movements in Ukraine or statements about regional security, and then exaggerated them into predictions of immediate conflict.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldova_and_the_Russo-Ukrainian_war" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Moldova and the Russo-Ukrainian war</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Moldova and the Russo-Ukrainian war</span></span></span>

The success of many of these stories depended less on evidence than on timing. During periods of heightened tension, people were already searching for information about security risks. False claims could therefore spread rapidly through social media channels before fact-checkers, journalists or officials could address them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wired.com/story/moldova-disinformation-war-ukraine" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Videos and posts, often originating on TikTok, are being amplified on Facebook to spread anti-refugee sentiment. ## Coup Stories, Saboteur Rumours and Political Anxiety

Another category of security scare involved rumours about coups, infiltrators and covert operations. In early 2023, Moldova’s leadership publicly discussed intelligence indicating plans to destabilise the government. Those allegations were treated seriously by Moldovan authorities and attracted international attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Moldovan_coup_attempt_allegations" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2023 Moldovan coup attempt allegations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">2023 Moldovan coup attempt allegations</span></span></span>

The existence of genuine security concerns created fertile ground for exaggeration and speculation. Online discussions often expanded beyond verified information, producing claims that ranged from plausible to highly improbable. Stories circulated about hidden saboteurs, foreign agents and imminent attempts to seize key institutions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Moldovan_coup_attempt_allegations" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2023 Moldovan coup attempt allegations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">2023 Moldovan coup attempt allegations</span></span></span>

A notable feature of these rumours was the difficulty of separating real warnings from embellished versions. ## How Fragments of Truth Made Rumours Believable

The most effective wartime rumours in Moldova rarely relied on complete fabrication. Instead, they mixed authentic events with misleading conclusions.

* **Real incidents, false interpretations.** Missile debris, airspace closures or security operations were genuine events, but rumours transformed them into evidence of invasion or secret military action.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/8d35f4ea80376c6e9828e79c7f195e22" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AP News Rocket debris found again in Moldova, from war next door</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The wreckage was found in Larga, Briceni county, near the Ukrainian border, prompting the deployment of a bomb squad and the area being c...</span></span></span>

## What These Security Scares Reveal

Moldova’s bomb hoaxes and wartime rumours illustrate a modern form of deception in which the goal is often disruption rather than long-term belief. A fake mobilisation notice does not need to remain credible for months if it generates panic for a few hours.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/fake-bombs-failed-coup-moldova-smolders-border-russias-war-2023-03-10/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fake bombs and failed coup: Moldova smolders on border</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fake bombs and failed coup: Moldova smolders on border...March 10, 2023 — 14 Mar 2023 — A coup attempt, bomb hoaxes, internet hac...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 10, 2023</span></span></span>
