## Introduction

In 2020, [Ghana]({{ 'how-ghanas-most-famous-hoaxes-won-belief/' | relative_url }}) became the centre of one of the country's most talked-about prestige scams when Kwame Owusu Fordjour, widely known as “Dr UN”, staged an awards ceremony that appeared to carry the backing of the United Nations and the legacy of former UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan. Musicians, media personalities, academics and other prominent figures accepted honours at a formal event in Accra, only for questions to emerge almost immediately about whether the awards had any genuine connection to the institutions being invoked. The episode was not a classic financial fraud aimed at ordinary victims. Instead, it was a lesson in how symbols of international authority, respectable venues and social proof can make an invented institution appear legitimate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.citinewsroom.com/2020/09/un-ghana-distances-itself-from-kwame-fordjours-fake-awards/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citinewsroom.com">[CitiNewsroom.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citinewsroom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Citi Newsroom.com U N Ghana distances itself from Kwame Fordjour&#x27;s &#x27;fake</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The United Nations office in Ghana has distanced itself from an award scheme set...Read more...</span></span></span>

## How the Ceremony Borrowed International Authority

The awards were presented under names such as the “Global Blueprint Excellence Award” and were promoted in ways that suggested links to the United Nations and to Kofi Annan. Organiser Kwame Owusu Fordjour cultivated the public persona of “Dr UN”, a title that itself implied an association with an internationally respected organisation. Promotional materials and public statements reportedly described the awards as connected to the UN and the Kofi Annan Foundation, claims that were central to the event’s credibility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.citinewsroom.com/2020/09/kwabena-brakopowers-writes-sarkodie-ug-vc-feeding-bottle-plaques-and-modern-award-scam/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citinewsroom.com">[CitiNewsroom.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citinewsroom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Citi Newsroom.com Kwabena Brakopowers writes: Sarkodie, UG VC, &#x27;feeding</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kwabena Brakopowers writes: Sarkodie, UG VC, &#x27;feeding...September 17, 2020 — Kwame Fordjour is reported to have said that the Global Blu...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 17, 2020</span></span></span>

The mechanics of the deception were surprisingly simple. Rather than forging an entire international organisation, the event borrowed visual and symbolic cues that people already trusted:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The organiser adopted an authoritative title.
* The ceremony was held in a respectable hotel venue.
* Recipients included well-known public figures.
* The awards were presented with formal staging, plaques and speeches.
* References to the United Nations and Kofi Annan supplied instant prestige.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.citinewsroom.com/2020/09/un-ghana-distances-itself-from-kwame-fordjours-fake-awards/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citinewsroom.com">[CitiNewsroom.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citinewsroom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Citi Newsroom.com U N Ghana distances itself from Kwame Fordjour&#x27;s &#x27;fake</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The United Nations office in Ghana has distanced itself from an award scheme set...Read more...</span></span></span>

The strategy exploited a common psychological shortcut. Most people do not investigate every institution they encounter. When an event looks professional, includes recognised personalities and invokes respected global bodies, many assume someone else has already checked its legitimacy.

## Why Prominent Recipients Trusted the Event

One of the most remarkable features of the scandal was the calibre of some recipients. Well-known musicians, broadcasters, journalists and public personalities attended and accepted awards. Among those publicly associated with the event were major Ghanaian entertainment and media figures whose presence then reinforced the appearance of legitimacy for everyone else involved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.pulse.com.gh/story/united-nations-disowns-kwame-fordjuors-fake-un-awards-2024081112441042902" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pulse.com.gh">[Pulse Ghana]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pulse.com.gh</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on com.gh.</span></span></span>

This is a classic example of social proof. People often judge credibility by observing who else has endorsed something. A guest arriving at a ceremony and seeing respected public figures in attendance may reasonably conclude that background checks have already been performed.

The event also benefited from the fact that honours and recognition schemes are common. Genuine awards frequently involve private organisations, charities, foundations and professional associations. Because many legitimate honours are not widely known before they are presented, an unfamiliar award does not automatically raise suspicion.

Later accounts from attendees suggested that some participants became doubtful only after arriving at the event or hearing parts of the proceedings. By then, however, the ceremony already had enough momentum that recipients continued to participate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ghheadlines.com/agency/3news/20240320/165208423/d-black-narrates-how-he-was-scammed-by-dr-uns-fake-awards" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ghheadlines.com">[GhHeadlines]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ghheadlines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gh Headlines D-Black narrates how he was scammed by Dr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UN&#x27;s fake...20 Mar 2024 — D-Black said as soon as Dr. UN begun giving his speech, he suspected the fake award scheme and wanted to leave...</span></span></span>

## The Moment the Story Unravelled

The deception began to collapse when journalists, social-media users and fact-checkers examined the claimed affiliations behind the awards. Attention quickly focused on whether the United Nations had authorised or recognised the programme.

The decisive blow came when the United Nations office in Ghana publicly distanced itself from the awards scheme. The organisation stated that it had no connection to the event or to the awards being distributed. Once the institution whose prestige had been borrowed denied involvement, the central claim supporting the awards’ authority largely disappeared.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.citinewsroom.com/2020/09/un-ghana-distances-itself-from-kwame-fordjours-fake-awards/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citinewsroom.com">[CitiNewsroom.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citinewsroom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Citi Newsroom.com U N Ghana distances itself from Kwame Fordjour&#x27;s &#x27;fake</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The United Nations office in Ghana has distanced itself from an award scheme set...Read more...</span></span></span>

Fact-checkers and commentators also scrutinised Fordjour’s public claims and background. ## What Made the Awards Seem Convincing

The Dr UN affair demonstrates that successful prestige scams rarely depend on a single fabricated document or outright lie. Instead, they assemble multiple signals that people instinctively associate with authenticity.

Several factors worked together:

**Institutional borrowing.** The United Nations is one of the world's most recognisable organisations. Simply suggesting a connection provided instant authority.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.citinewsroom.com/2020/09/un-ghana-distances-itself-from-kwame-fordjours-fake-awards/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citinewsroom.com">[CitiNewsroom.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citinewsroom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Citi Newsroom.com U N Ghana distances itself from Kwame Fordjour&#x27;s &#x27;fake</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The United Nations office in Ghana has distanced itself from an award scheme set...Read more...</span></span></span>

**Association with respected individuals.** Invoking Kofi Annan, one of Ghana’s most internationally admired figures, added moral and historical weight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.citinewsroom.com/2020/09/kwabena-brakopowers-writes-sarkodie-ug-vc-feeding-bottle-plaques-and-modern-award-scam/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citinewsroom.com">[CitiNewsroom.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citinewsroom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Citi Newsroom.com Kwabena Brakopowers writes: Sarkodie, UG VC, &#x27;feeding</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kwabena Brakopowers writes: Sarkodie, UG VC, &#x27;feeding...September 17, 2020 — Kwame Fordjour is reported to have said that the Global Blu...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 17, 2020</span></span></span>

**Professional presentation.** Formal ceremonies, awards plaques and a respectable venue created the atmosphere of an established institution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/TheGhanaWeb/posts/although-kwame-owusu-fordjour-dr-un-has-been-rebuked-and-ridiculed-by-a-section-/5840386322705384/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[Facebook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Although Kwame Owusu Fordjour (Dr UN) has been...The fake awards titled the &#x27;Global Blueprint Excellence Award&#x27; was held two wee...</span></span></span>

**Network effects.** Every prominent attendee made the event look more credible to the next attendee. Social proof accumulated throughout the process.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.pulse.com.gh/story/united-nations-disowns-kwame-fordjuors-fake-un-awards-2024081112441042902" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pulse.com.gh">[Pulse Ghana]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pulse.com.gh</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on com.gh.</span></span></span>

These mechanisms are common in confidence schemes. Rather than persuading people through evidence, they persuade through appearances, reputation and perceived consensus.

## What the Scandal Revealed About Social Proof

The lasting significance of the case is not that a fake award ceremony existed. Counterfeit honours and dubious recognition schemes appear in many countries. What made the Ghanaian episode memorable was how clearly it exposed the power of social proof among educated and influential people.

The scandal showed that credibility often flows through networks rather than through direct verification. Individuals may trust an event because respected colleagues are attending. Those colleagues may trust it because other respected figures have already accepted invitations. In such situations, very few participants independently check the underlying claims.

The affair also highlighted a broader lesson about prestige itself. Awards derive value from the reputation of the institutions behind them. When that reputation is merely borrowed rather than earned, the appearance of legitimacy can survive only until someone asks a basic question: who actually authorised the honour?

## A Place in Ghana's History of Modern Hoaxes

Within Ghana’s wider history of deception and imposture, the Dr UN episode stands out as a distinctly twenty-first-century case. It was not based on hidden treasure, forged antiquities or supernatural claims. Instead, it relied on branding, public relations and the symbolic power of international institutions.

The scandal demonstrated how easily authority can be simulated through titles, ceremonies and carefully chosen associations. It also showed the importance of independent verification in an age when prestige itself can be manufactured.
