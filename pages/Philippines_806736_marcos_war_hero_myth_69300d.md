## Introduction

Ferdinand Marcos really did serve during the Second World War. The controversy is not whether he was a veteran, but whether his wartime record was transformed into a political legend that went far beyond the available evidence. For decades Marcos was celebrated as the [Philippines]({{ 'philippines/' | relative_url }})' greatest war hero, a man said to have led a large guerrilla army, won dozens of medals, and performed extraordinary feats against Japanese forces. Those claims became a powerful part of his political identity and helped support his rise from congressman to senator, president, and eventually dictator.

Yet military investigations, archival records, and later historical research found major contradictions in many of the stories that made up the Marcos wartime legend. American and Philippine records repeatedly questioned his claims about guerrilla leadership, decorations, and combat achievements. What emerged was not a simple case of an invented biography, but a striking example of how political propaganda, selective storytelling, and official repetition can turn a disputed personal history into an accepted national myth.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP90-00965R000302300014-2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[cia.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MARCOS&#x27;S WARTIME ROLE DISCREDITED IN U.S. FILESMarcos has claimed that Maharlika was a force of. 8,200 men. ## The Claims About Medals and Maharlika

The centrepiece of Marcos's wartime reputation was his claim that he had been one of the most highly decorated soldiers in Philippine history. During his political career he asserted that he had received around thirty military decorations, including prestigious American awards such as the Distinguished Service Cross and other honours associated with exceptional bravery. He also claimed to have performed heroic actions during the defence of Bataan and to have received recognition from senior Allied commanders.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_career_of_Ferdinand_Marcos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Military career of Ferdinand Marcos</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Military career of Ferdinand Marcos</span></span></span>

Alongside the medals story was an even larger claim. Marcos said that after escaping Japanese captivity he organised and led a guerrilla force known as Ang Mga Maharlika in northern Luzon. According to his accounts, this force numbered thousands of fighters and played an important role in resistance operations against the Japanese occupation. The Maharlika story became a key part of the image of Marcos as a fearless wartime leader.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_career_of_Ferdinand_Marcos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Military career of Ferdinand Marcos</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Military career of Ferdinand Marcos</span></span></span>

The problem was that these claims repeatedly ran into difficulties when checked against military records. American Army investigators reviewing guerrilla recognition claims after the war found serious problems with the Maharlika narrative. Several investigations concluded that the organisation either did not exist in the form Marcos described or had been greatly exaggerated. Internal military correspondence questioned the authenticity of claims submitted on behalf of the unit and rejected attempts to secure official recognition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.upi.com/Archives/1986/01/23/The-Army-twice-denied-Philippine-President-Ferdinand-Marcos-claims/6433506840400/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upi.com">[upi.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Army twice denied Philippine President Ferdinand</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Army twice denied Philippine President Ferdinand... - UPI23 Jan 1986 — The Army twice denied Philippine President Ferdinand Marco...</span></span></span>

The medals story also faced scrutiny. Researchers examining wartime records found no evidence for several of the most prestigious decorations Marcos later claimed. Historians and the National Historical Commission of the Philippines concluded that some of the American awards cited in Marcos's biographies were unsupported by official military documentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/804746/marcos-military-record-full-of-lies-says-nhcp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newsinfo.inquirer.net">[inquirer.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newsinfo.inquirer.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">marcos military record full of lies says nhcp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inquirer.netMarcos&#x27; military record full of lies, says NHCP &#124; Inquirer News10 Aug 2016 — Ferdinand Marcos lied about receiving three mili...</span></span></span>

## How the Legend Supported Political Power

The significance of the wartime narrative was political as much as historical. Marcos entered national politics in the years after independence, when memories of the Japanese occupation remained vivid and wartime sacrifice carried enormous prestige. A candidate presented as a heroic resistance leader enjoyed an advantage that ordinary politicians could not easily match.

By the 1960s Marcos was promoting himself as the country's most decorated war hero. The image appeared in campaign materials, speeches, biographies, and media coverage. The heroic reputation helped portray him as courageous, disciplined, patriotic, and uniquely qualified for national leadership. It also provided a dramatic personal story that distinguished him from rivals.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_career_of_Ferdinand_Marcos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Military career of Ferdinand Marcos</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Military career of Ferdinand Marcos</span></span></span>

Once Marcos became president, the legend acquired the weight of state authority. Official biographies, government publications, and supportive media often repeated the claims. In an era when access to military archives was limited and public fact-checking mechanisms were weaker, repetition itself became a source of credibility. The story was not simply a campaign narrative; it became part of the broader mythology surrounding Marcos's rule.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ortigasfoundationlibrary.com.ph/articles/stolen-valor-world-war-ii-saga-ferdinand-e-marcos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ortigasfoundationlibrary.com.ph">[Ortigas Foundation Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ortigasfoundationlibrary.com.ph</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ortigas Foundation Library Stolen Valor: World War II Saga of Ferdinand E</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Marcos27 Oct 2021 — In 1945, he sent this fairytale to the US military and was rejected as having no facts to substantiate his claims. This pattern resembles other famous Philippine historical myths. A claim gains legitimacy because it serves a political or cultural purpose, receives endorsement from powerful institutions, and is repeated so often that questioning it begins to seem unreasonable. In the Marcos case, the wartime hero image reinforced the broader narrative that he was an exceptional leader destined to guide the nation.

## What Military Records and Historians Found

The most damaging challenges to the wartime legend came from official records rather than political opponents. American military investigators reviewed Marcos's wartime submissions several times during the late 1940s. Documents later released from archives showed investigators rejecting key elements of his claims and describing some submissions as false or misleading.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1986-01-24-mn-23754-story.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: latimes.com">[Los Angeles Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">latimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">la xpm 1986 01 24 mn 23754 story</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">la xpm 1986 01 24 mn 23754 story</span></span></span>

One recurring issue involved the Maharlika guerrilla unit. Investigators found insufficient evidence that the organisation operated as Marcos claimed. Army reviews repeatedly refused to recognise the force in the manner he sought. Later examinations of declassified files found officials concluding that many of the claims connected with the unit lacked documentary support.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newslab.philstar.com/31-years-of-amnesia/war-hero" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newslab.philstar.com">[philstar.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newslab.philstar.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Imagined heroism &#124; 31 years of amnesiaRecognition by the US Army as a guerilla meant getting back pay, and the... Questions also emerged about specific stories of battlefield heroism. Historians who examined operational records found little contemporary evidence for some of the dramatic exploits later associated with Marcos. Officers who served in relevant units did not always corroborate the accounts that appeared in later biographies. Researchers likewise noted the absence of documentation supporting several claimed decorations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_career_of_Ferdinand_Marcos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Military career of Ferdinand Marcos</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Military career of Ferdinand Marcos</span></span></span>

The issue attracted renewed attention in 1986, when archival research by journalists and historians brought previously obscure military documents into public view. Reports based on United States Army files described extensive discrepancies between Marcos's public image and the records held by military authorities. These revelations appeared during the final months of his presidency, when broader scrutiny of the regime was already intensifying.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP90-00965R000302300014-2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[CIA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MARCOS&#x27;S WARTIME ROLE DISCREDITED IN U.S. FILESMarcos has claimed that Maharlika was a force of. 8,200 men. ## Why So Many People Believed It

The Marcos war myth succeeded because it contained elements of truth. Marcos had military training, served during the defence of the Philippines, and experienced the war firsthand. The disputed claims were built upon a real wartime record rather than invented from nothing. That made exaggerations harder to detect.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ferdinand Marcos</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ferdinand Marcos</span></span></span>

The story also fit public expectations. Many nations emerging from war celebrate heroic veterans, and post-war Philippine politics often rewarded military credentials. A narrative about a brave young officer leading resistance fighters was emotionally compelling and politically useful.

Access to evidence was another factor. The archival records that later challenged Marcos's claims were not widely known for many years. By the time contradictory documents became public, the heroic reputation had already been embedded in textbooks, biographies, and public memory. ## A Propaganda Legend That Outlived Its Exposure

The Marcos wartime story occupies an unusual place in Philippine history because it sits between genuine service and manufactured glory. Most historians do not argue that Marcos invented his entire wartime experience. The dispute concerns the scale of his achievements and the credibility of the honours and exploits later attached to his name.

As a case study in political myth-making, it remains one of the Philippines' most important examples of how a reputation can be amplified into legend. The story demonstrates how official recognition, media repetition, and personal ambition can reshape public memory, even when archival evidence points in a different direction.
