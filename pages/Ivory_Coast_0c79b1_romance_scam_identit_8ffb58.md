## Introduction

Among the best-documented forms of modern deception linked to [Ivory Coast]({{ 'how-false-stories-took-hold-in-ivory/' | relative_url }}) are romance scams built on entirely fabricated online identities. Unlike a simple confidence trick, these schemes depended on long conversations, emotional investment and carefully constructed digital personas. Victims were persuaded that they were speaking to a soldier overseas, a widowed professional, a successful entrepreneur or a celebrity admirer. In reality, the profile photographs were often stolen, the biographies invented and the relationship itself fictional. Researchers, police investigations and international cybercrime operations have repeatedly identified Ivorian-based fraud networks involved in romance fraud and related forms of sexual blackmail known as sextortion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949791424000083" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unravelling the organisation of ivorian cyberfraudstersby C Cretu-Adatte · 2024 · Cited by 25 — This article delves into the...</span></span></span>

The significance of these cases lies not only in the money stolen. They reveal how internet-era hoaxes can create entire fictional people. ## How a Convincing Identity Was Assembled

The foundation of the fraud was the invented persona. Investigations into Ivorian cyberfraud networks describe the use of false profiles built from photographs taken from social media, dating sites or public image collections. These profiles were then given plausible biographies designed to attract trust and sympathy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949791424000083" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unravelling the organisation of ivorian cyberfraudstersby C Cretu-Adatte · 2024 · Cited by 25 — This article delves into the...</span></span></span>

A typical fabricated identity included several elements:

* A stolen portrait that appeared attractive, respectable or successful.
* A detailed backstory involving work abroad, military service, business travel or family tragedy.
* Social-media accounts populated with copied images and carefully managed interactions.
* A willingness to communicate frequently and patiently over long periods.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/scams-and-safety/common-frauds-and-scams/romance-scams" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Romance ScamsIn romance scams, a criminal uses a fake online identity to gain a victim&#x27;s affection and trust. </div>

What made these personas persuasive was not technological sophistication but consistency. The scammer maintained the same fictional character across messages, emails and social-media platforms. Victims often interpreted this apparent continuity as proof that the person was real.

Researchers studying Ivorian cyberfraudsters, commonly known as *brouteurs*, found that romance scams relied heavily on emotional performance. The goal was to create trust before any financial request appeared. Fraudsters invested significant time in conversation because the relationship itself was the mechanism that made later demands believable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949791424000083" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unravelling the organisation of ivorian cyberfraudstersby C Cretu-Adatte · 2024 · Cited by 25 — This article delves into the...</span></span></span>

## Why Emergencies and Intimacy Created Leverage

Once trust had been established, the fictional relationship entered a second stage. The invented partner suddenly faced a crisis that supposedly required urgent assistance. This pattern appears repeatedly across romance-fraud investigations worldwide and is specifically identified in research on Ivorian romance-scam operations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10610-024-09597-7" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: link.springer.com">[Springer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">link.springer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">How do Ivorian Cyberfraudsters Manage Their Criminal...by C Cretu-Adatte · 2024 · Cited by 16 — Sextortion, with initial stages...</span></span></span>

The emergency might involve:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* A medical problem.
* Travel difficulties.
* Customs fees.
* Frozen bank accounts.
* Legal troubles.
* Lost luggage or stolen property.

</div>

The victim was encouraged to see the payment not as a business transaction but as proof of love, loyalty or commitment. Because the relationship had already become emotionally important, requests that might otherwise seem suspicious could appear reasonable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/scams-and-safety/common-frauds-and-scams/romance-scams" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Romance ScamsIn romance scams, a criminal uses a fake online identity to gain a victim&#x27;s affection and trust. A related variation moved from romance to blackmail. Instead of seeking money through emergencies, the fraudster persuaded the victim to share intimate photographs or participate in explicit video conversations. Once images had been obtained, the relationship abruptly changed. Affection was replaced by threats to publish the material unless payment was made. Researchers note that sextortion often begins with the same trust-building process as a romance scam before shifting into coercion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10610-024-09597-7" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: link.springer.com">[Springer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">link.springer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">How do Ivorian Cyberfraudsters Manage Their Criminal...by C Cretu-Adatte · 2024 · Cited by 16 — Sextortion, with initial stages...</span></span></span>

## How Organised Roles Supported Fraud and Blackmail

Popular depictions sometimes imagine a lone scammer working from a laptop. Evidence from studies of Ivorian cyberfraud suggests a more organised reality. Researchers examining the structure of *brouteur* networks found that romance fraud frequently involved cooperation, mentoring and specialised roles rather than entirely isolated offenders.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949791424000083" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unravelling the organisation of ivorian cyberfraudstersby C Cretu-Adatte · 2024 · Cited by 25 — This article delves into the...</span></span></span>

Different participants might contribute different skills:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Creating convincing social-media profiles.
* Collecting photographs and identity material.
* Managing conversations with victims.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.euronews.com/2025/09/26/at-least-260-suspects-in-online-romance-scams-arrested-across-africa-interpol-says" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: euronews.com">[euronews.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">euronews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>
* Receiving and moving funds.
* Conducting blackmail once compromising images had been obtained.

</div>

This division of labour helped sustain multiple fictional relationships simultaneously. A victim might believe they were engaged in a unique personal connection while the operators behind the profile were managing numerous identities at once.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949791424000083" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unravelling the organisation of ivorian cyberfraudstersby C Cretu-Adatte · 2024 · Cited by 25 — This article delves into the...</span></span></span>

The organised nature of these activities became clearer through international investigations. ## How Investigators Exposed the Fiction

The weakness of the fabricated identity was that it depended on borrowed evidence. Investigators often exposed scams through techniques that were surprisingly simple.

Reverse-image searches revealed that profile photographs belonged to unrelated people. Social-media timelines showed inconsistencies. Claimed occupations could not be verified. Supposed military personnel, engineers or international business figures often had no traceable professional existence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/scams-and-safety/common-frauds-and-scams/romance-scams" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Romance ScamsIn romance scams, a criminal uses a fake online identity to gain a victim&#x27;s affection and trust.
