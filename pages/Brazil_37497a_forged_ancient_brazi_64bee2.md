## Introduction

Did Phoenicians really reach ancient [Brazil]({{ 'how-brazils-most-famous-hoaxes-took-hold/' | relative_url }})? The most famous piece of evidence ever offered for that claim is the Paraíba inscription, a supposed Phoenician text reported in north-eastern Brazil in 1872. If genuine, it would suggest that Mediterranean sailors crossed the Atlantic nearly two thousand years before Columbus. Yet the inscription has long occupied a curious place in the history of hoaxes and pseudohistory: it is one of the best-known examples of how a missing artefact, an exciting translation and weak provenance can create a remarkably durable alternative history. Scholars today overwhelmingly regard the inscription as a nineteenth-century forgery, but the story continues to reappear in books, documentaries and lost-civilisation theories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/43078827" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jstor.org">[jstor.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jstor.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Phoenician Inscription from Brazil.by FM Cross · 1968 · Cited by 39 — The Phoenician Inscription from Brazil. The case matters because it illustrates a recurring pattern in claims about Brazil's ancient past. Extraordinary discoveries are announced, the original object is unavailable or poorly documented, and speculation expands into grand narratives about forgotten visitors from the Old World. The Paraíba inscription remains the classic example.

## What the Paraíba inscription claimed

The story began when Ladislau Netto, director of Brazil's National Museum, received a copy of an inscription said to have been found on a stone in Paraíba province. The original stone was never produced for scholarly examination. What survived was a transcription of the text, allegedly copied from the monument.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://library.biblicalarchaeology.org/article/phoenicians-in-brazil/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: library.biblicalarchaeology.org">[biblicalarchaeology.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">library.biblicalarchaeology.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The BAS Library Phoenicians in Brazil?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The BAS LibraryThe Paraiba inscription is said to be a Phoenician inscription carved in stone and found in northeastern Brazil in 1872. Translated as Phoenician, the inscription described sailors from Sidon who had supposedly been driven far off course after a maritime expedition. According to the text, they ended up on a distant mountainous shore after a disastrous voyage. To believers, the inscription appeared to offer direct testimony that Phoenician mariners had reached the coast of South America around the sixth century BCE.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0%2C33009%2C844460%2C00.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: content.time.com">[TIME]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">content.time.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archaeology: Before Columbus or the Vikings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archaeology: Before Columbus or the Vikings - TIMEChiseled into a crude stone tablet in the language of the ancient Phoenicians, the...</span></span></span>

The claim arrived at a moment when scholars and the public were fascinated by ancient exploration. Nineteenth-century archaeology was rapidly expanding, and discoveries from Egypt and the Near East regularly captured international attention. A text linking Brazil to the ancient Mediterranean seemed capable of rewriting world history. In a young nation still constructing historical narratives about its origins, the possibility of a dramatic pre-Columbian connection carried obvious appeal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://library.biblicalarchaeology.org/article/a-cryptogram-in-the-phoenician-inscription-from-brazil/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: library.biblicalarchaeology.org">[The BAS Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">library.biblicalarchaeology.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">a cryptogram in the phoenician inscription from brazil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The BAS LibraryA Cryptogram in the Phoenician Inscription from BrazilTo understand how circumstances in the 1870&#x27;s led the forger of the...</span></span></span>

## How scholars identified the forgery

The first major problem was simple: nobody could inspect the artefact itself. The stone vanished from the historical record almost immediately. Investigators were left with a copied text whose origin could not be verified. Attempts to locate the discoverer or recover the original object failed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_Phoenician_discovery_of_the_Americas" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Theory of Phoenician discovery of the Americas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Theory of Phoenician discovery of the Americas</span></span></span>

Even before the twentieth century, specialists noticed difficulties. The French philologist Ernest Renan, one of the leading authorities on Phoenician studies, concluded that the inscription was not authentic. His criticism helped persuade Netto to abandon his initial acceptance of the discovery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.biblicalarchaeology.org/daily/phoenicians-sailing-to-the-new-world/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: biblicalarchaeology.org">[biblicalarchaeology.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">biblicalarchaeology.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Biblical Archaeology SocietyPhoenicians Sailing to the New WorldWith regard to the Paraiba Stone and inscription, the French historian an...</span></span></span>

The most influential modern analysis came from Harvard epigrapher Frank Moore Cross in 1968. Cross examined the text in detail and argued that it displayed exactly the sort of mistakes expected from a nineteenth-century forger working from published reference books rather than from genuine ancient practice. His study concluded that the inscription was a modern fabrication.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/43078827" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jstor.org">[JSTOR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jstor.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Phoenician Inscription from Brazil.by FM Cross · 1968 · Cited by 39 — The Phoenician Inscription from Brazil. A Nineteenth-Centu...</span></span></span>

Several features raised suspicion:

* **Mixed letter forms:** Characters from different historical phases of the Phoenician alphabet appeared together in ways that genuine inscriptions do not. Some forms belonged to periods separated by centuries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/43078827" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jstor.org">[jstor.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jstor.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Phoenician Inscription from Brazil.by FM Cross · 1968 · Cited by 39 — The Phoenician Inscription from Brazil. A Nineteenth-Centu...</span></span></span>
* **Anachronistic language:** Linguistic features seemed assembled from scholarly sources rather than reflecting a naturally written ancient text.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/43078827" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jstor.org">[JSTOR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jstor.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Phoenician Inscription from Brazil.by FM Cross · 1968 · Cited by 39 — The Phoenician Inscription from Brazil. A Nineteenth-Centu...</span></span></span>
* **Suspicious preservation:** The text appeared unusually complete and convenient despite supposedly surviving a long and difficult history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.biblicalarchaeology.org/daily/phoenicians-sailing-to-the-new-world/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: biblicalarchaeology.org">[Biblical Archaeology Society]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">biblicalarchaeology.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Biblical Archaeology SocietyPhoenicians Sailing to the New WorldWith regard to the Paraiba Stone and inscription, the French historian an...</span></span></span>
* **Missing archaeological context:** There was no documented excavation, no verified discovery site and no surviving object available for testing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://library.biblicalarchaeology.org/article/phoenicians-in-brazil/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: library.biblicalarchaeology.org">[biblicalarchaeology.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">library.biblicalarchaeology.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The BAS Library Phoenicians in Brazil?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The BAS LibraryThe Paraiba inscription is said to be a Phoenician inscription carved in stone and found in northeastern Brazil in 1872. A...</span></span></span>

In archaeology, context is often as important as the object itself. An artefact without a secure chain of custody can rarely support revolutionary historical conclusions. The Paraíba inscription failed that basic test.

## Why the debate did not disappear

Although most specialists accepted the forgery diagnosis, the story never completely vanished. Part of the reason is that ancient transoceanic contact is not inherently impossible. Historians recognise that ancient peoples were capable sailors, and genuine questions remain about the extent of pre-Columbian navigation in different parts of the world. Because the broader question is interesting, weak evidence can sometimes survive longer than it otherwise would.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.biblicalarchaeology.org/daily/phoenicians-sailing-to-the-new-world/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: biblicalarchaeology.org">[Biblical Archaeology Society]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">biblicalarchaeology.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Biblical Archaeology SocietyPhoenicians Sailing to the New WorldWith regard to the Paraiba Stone and inscription, the French historian an...</span></span></span>

The Paraíba inscription also benefited from a common feature of pseudohistorical claims: the disappearance of the crucial object. For believers, the missing stone can be interpreted as evidence of suppression or neglect. For historians, however, the absence of the original artefact is a major reason for scepticism. The same fact is interpreted in opposite ways by different audiences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nephicode.blogspot.com/2016/10/the-paraiba-inscriptions-and-two-south.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nephicode.blogspot.com">[nephicode.blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nephicode.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">A text which tells of “Sidonian</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Paraiba Inscriptions and the Two South America...Oct 2, 2016 — The inscription itself is lost, and all that exists (if there ever wa...</span></span></span>

A further complication is that a minority of researchers periodically attempted to defend the inscription's authenticity. These challenges kept the controversy alive long after most epigraphers considered the matter settled. The result is a debate that appears more balanced in popular literature than it does within mainstream scholarship.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.badarchaeology.com/out-of-place-artefacts/petroglyphs-inscriptions-and-reliefs/the-paraiba-parahyba-stone/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: badarchaeology.com">[badarchaeology.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">badarchaeology.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the paraiba parahyba stone</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">the paraiba parahyba stone</span></span></span>

## How the inscription shaped Brazil's invented ancient past

The Paraíba inscription became a template for later claims about mysterious Old World visitors to Brazil. Once the idea entered popular culture, it encouraged searches for supporting traces elsewhere.

Natural rock formations were interpreted as inscriptions. Eroded shapes were described as carvings. Isolated marks became evidence of Phoenicians, Carthaginians or other ancient visitors. One well-known example is the speculation surrounding Pedra da Gávea near Rio de Janeiro, where alleged inscriptions and a supposed carved face have been repeatedly cited as proof of Phoenician presence despite geological and archaeological explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_interest_of_Pedra_da_G%C3%A1vea" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archaeological interest of Pedra da Gávea</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archaeological interest of Pedra da Gávea</span></span></span>

## Why lost-civilisation stories still return

The enduring appeal of the Paraíba inscription says less about ancient Phoenicians than about modern audiences. Lost-civilisation stories offer a dramatic alternative to established history. They promise hidden knowledge, forgotten voyages and revelations capable of overturning textbooks.

Brazil provides fertile ground for such stories because of its vast territory, rich Indigenous heritage and many regions that were historically understudied by archaeology. Genuine mysteries and gaps in knowledge can easily be filled by imaginative speculation. Yet the Paraíba case demonstrates why professional archaeologists place such emphasis on excavation records, provenance and independent verification. Without those safeguards, it becomes difficult to distinguish a remarkable discovery from a convincing invention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/43078827" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jstor.org">[jstor.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jstor.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Phoenician Inscription from Brazil.by FM Cross · 1968 · Cited by 39 — The Phoenician Inscription from Brazil.
