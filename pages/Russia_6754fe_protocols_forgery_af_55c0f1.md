## Introduction

Few forgeries have had consequences as far-reaching as *The Protocols of the Elders of Zion*. First circulated in the late Russian Empire, the document claimed to reveal secret plans by Jewish leaders to dominate governments, finance, the press and public life. It presented itself as the minutes of a clandestine meeting and offered what appeared to be documentary proof for long-standing antisemitic suspicions. In reality, it was a fabrication assembled from earlier political and fictional works. Yet exposure did not destroy its influence. Instead, the forgery became one of the most widely distributed conspiracy texts in modern history, shaping political movements far beyond [Russia]({{ 'how-russias-most-famous-deceptions-took/' | relative_url }}) and surviving repeated debunkings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/protocols-of-the-elders-of-zion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.ushmm.org">[Holocaust Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.ushmm.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">protocols of the elders of zion</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Holocaust EncyclopediaAn Antisemitic Conspiracy: The Protocols of the Elders of Zion23 Apr 2026 — The following year, The Times newspaper...</span></span></span>

The story matters not only because the document was false, but because it demonstrates how a forgery can acquire a life of its own. Once people began treating it as evidence rather than propaganda, the text became remarkably resistant to correction. Its afterlife reveals how conspiracy narratives can persist even when their origins are publicly exposed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ushmm.org/en/information/exhibitions/museum-exhibitions/a-dangerous-lie-the-protocols-of-the-elders-of-zion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ushmm.org">[United States Holocaust Memorial Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ushmm.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">United States Holocaust Memorial MuseumA Dangerous Lie: The Protocols of the Elders of ZionThe Holocaust, the state-sponsored persecution...</span></span></span>

## What the forged document claimed

The *Protocols* purported to be the record of secret meetings at which Jewish leaders discussed a plan for world domination. According to the text, the conspirators intended to manipulate financial systems, influence newspapers, undermine traditional institutions, provoke political unrest and eventually establish global control. The document offered no verifiable evidence for these claims, but its presentation as leaked minutes gave it an appearance of authenticity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/protocols-of-the-elders-of-zion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.ushmm.org">[Holocaust Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.ushmm.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">protocols of the elders of zion</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Holocaust EncyclopediaAn Antisemitic Conspiracy: The Protocols of the Elders of Zion23 Apr 2026 — The following year, The Times newspaper...</span></span></span>

Its power lay less in originality than in packaging. Antisemitic myths about hidden Jewish influence already existed across Europe. The forgery transformed prejudice into something that looked like documentary proof. Readers were no longer being asked merely to distrust Jews; they were being shown what appeared to be the conspirators’ own written plans. That shift from rumour to apparent evidence helped explain the text's appeal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/protocols-of-the-elders-of-zion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.ushmm.org">[Holocaust Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.ushmm.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">protocols of the elders of zion</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Holocaust EncyclopediaAn Antisemitic Conspiracy: The Protocols of the Elders of Zion23 Apr 2026 — The following year, The Times newspaper...</span></span></span>

The document emerged in the turbulent political atmosphere of the Russian Empire at the beginning of the twentieth century. Although scholars continue to debate some details of authorship, many historians have connected its production to circles associated with the Tsarist secret police and anti-revolutionary politics. The forgery offered a convenient explanation for social and political change by attributing complex events to a single hidden enemy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okhrana" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

## How borrowed text became conspiracy evidence

The most devastating blow to the *Protocols* came not from a secret archive or confession but from a comparison of texts. Investigators discovered that large portions had been copied almost verbatim from Maurice Joly's 1864 French political satire *Dialogue in Hell Between Machiavelli and Montesquieu*. Joly's work attacked Napoleon III and contained no Jewish conspiracy. Passages were simply adapted, with Jewish conspirators replacing the original political targets.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/protocols-of-the-elders-of-zion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.ushmm.org">[Holocaust Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.ushmm.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">protocols of the elders of zion</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Holocaust EncyclopediaAn Antisemitic Conspiracy: The Protocols of the Elders of Zion23 Apr 2026 — The following year, The Times newspaper...</span></span></span>

The exposure became famous through a series of articles in *The Times* of London in 1921. Journalist Philip Graves placed passages from Joly's book alongside corresponding sections of the *Protocols*, demonstrating extensive plagiarism. The similarities were too close to dismiss as coincidence. Investigations also identified material apparently borrowed from Hermann Goedsche's 1868 novel *Biarritz*, another fictional source that had already contributed to conspiracy folklore.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thetimes.com/uk/article/jewish-world-plot-a-forgery-0jknwkr3m" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thetimes.com">[thetimes.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thetimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">jewish world plot a forgery 0jknwkr3m</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Times&#x27;Jewish world plot&#x27; a forgery16 Aug 2021 —... forgery. The significance of this discovery was straightforward. If the supposed secret minutes had been copied from earlier literary works, they could not be authentic records of a conspiracy meeting. The central claim of the document collapsed. By the early 1920s, the forgery had been publicly exposed through documentary comparison rather than speculation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/protocols-of-the-elders-of-zion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.ushmm.org">[Holocaust Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.ushmm.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">protocols of the elders of zion</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Holocaust EncyclopediaAn Antisemitic Conspiracy: The Protocols of the Elders of Zion23 Apr 2026 — The following year, The Times newspaper...</span></span></span>

## Why exposure failed to end its influence

The remarkable feature of the *Protocols* is not that they fooled people initially but that they continued to circulate after the fraud was demonstrated. Several factors helped explain this survival.

First, the forgery often appealed to readers who already accepted the underlying conspiracy theory. For such audiences, evidence of plagiarism could be dismissed as irrelevant, fabricated or part of a larger cover-up. The document's value became symbolic rather than evidential.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://academic.oup.com/hgs/article/29/2/212/562402" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academic.oup.com">[OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academic.oup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">OUP AcademicThe Protocols of the Elders of Zion in Nazi Propaganda, 1933...by RL Bytwerk · 2015 · Cited by 56 — They are based on a forg...</span></span></span>

Second, the upheavals surrounding the Russian Revolution gave the forgery a new audience. Anti-Bolshevik émigrés promoted the text internationally, portraying revolutionary events as part of a supposed Jewish plan for world domination. Linking the forgery to fears about communism dramatically increased its reach outside Russia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://florida.adl.org/resources/backgrounder/hoax-hate-protocols-learned-elders-zion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: florida.adl.org">[Florida ADL]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">florida.adl.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">hoax hate protocols learned elders zion</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">hoax hate protocols learned elders zion</span></span></span>

Third, conspiracy theories often provide emotionally satisfying explanations for complex events. Economic crises, political instability and rapid social change can appear easier to understand when attributed to a single hidden cause. The *Protocols* offered precisely that sort of explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ushmm.org/en/information/exhibitions/museum-exhibitions/a-dangerous-lie-the-protocols-of-the-elders-of-zion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ushmm.org">[United States Holocaust Memorial Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ushmm.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">United States Holocaust Memorial MuseumA Dangerous Lie: The Protocols of the Elders of ZionThe Holocaust, the state-sponsored persecution...</span></span></span>

The result was a paradox: exposure damaged the document's credibility among scholars and journalists, yet did not eliminate its usefulness as propaganda.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/protocols-of-the-elders-of-zion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.ushmm.org">[Holocaust Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.ushmm.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">protocols of the elders of zion</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Holocaust EncyclopediaAn Antisemitic Conspiracy: The Protocols of the Elders of Zion23 Apr 2026 — The following year, The Times newspaper...</span></span></span>

## From Russian forgery to global propaganda

The *Protocols* escaped their Russian origins and became an international political weapon. English translations appeared soon after the First World War, helping the text reach readers in Britain, the United States and elsewhere. Campaigners who wished to portray social change, financial modernisation or revolutionary politics as parts of a single conspiracy found the forgery a convenient tool.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.adl.org/resources/backgrounder/hoax-hate-protocols-learned-elders-zion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: adl.org">[ADL]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">adl.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">hoax hate protocols learned elders zion</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">hoax hate protocols learned elders zion</span></span></span>

Its most notorious adoption came in Nazi Germany. Nazi propagandists treated the document as confirmation of their antisemitic worldview despite the growing body of evidence demonstrating that it was fabricated. The forgery helped reinforce claims that Jews posed a coordinated threat to Germany and Europe. Historians of Nazi propaganda have shown that references to the *Protocols* continued even after their fraudulent origins were widely known.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://academic.oup.com/hgs/article/29/2/212/562402" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academic.oup.com">[OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academic.oup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">OUP AcademicThe Protocols of the Elders of Zion in Nazi Propaganda, 1933...by RL Bytwerk · 2015 · Cited by 56 — They are based on a forg...</span></span></span>

The document's influence did not end with the defeat of Nazi Germany. Various extremist movements, conspiracy theorists and propagandists continued to republish or reference it in different regions of the world.
