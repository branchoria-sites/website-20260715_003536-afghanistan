## Introduction

In [Lesotho]({{ 'how-false-stories-about-lesotho-took/' | relative_url }}), some of the most significant modern deception campaigns have not involved forged documents or invented legends. Instead, they have involved the theft of public trust. Fraudsters have increasingly used the faces, voices and reputations of the country’s most recognisable figures—King Letsie III, Prime Minister Sam Matekane and well-known broadcasters—to make dubious investment schemes appear official. The aim was not political persuasion but financial gain: convincing ordinary people that a risky or fraudulent opportunity had been endorsed by trusted national authorities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lesotho.misa.org/2025/07/22/fact-check-viral-video-of-king-letsie-iii-promoting-investment-scheme-is-a-deepfake-scam/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lesotho.misa.org">[MISA Lesotho]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lesotho.misa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lesotho FACT-CHECK: Viral video of King Letsie III promoting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MISA LesothoFACT-CHECK: Viral video of King Letsie III promoting...July 22, 2025 — 22 Jul 2025 — A viral deepfake video falsely claims t...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 22, 2025</span></span></span>

## The King's Deepfake Investment Promotion

One of the clearest examples emerged in 2025 when a video circulated online appearing to show King Letsie III endorsing a government-backed investment platform. The promotion promised weekly earnings of 19,320 loti and presented the offer as an opportunity supported by national leadership. For many viewers, the apparent endorsement from the monarch was the central selling point.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lesotho.misa.org/2025/07/22/fact-check-viral-video-of-king-letsie-iii-promoting-investment-scheme-is-a-deepfake-scam/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lesotho.misa.org">[MISA Lesotho]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lesotho.misa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lesotho FACT-CHECK: Viral video of King Letsie III promoting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MISA LesothoFACT-CHECK: Viral video of King Letsie III promoting...July 22, 2025 — 22 Jul 2025 — A viral deepfake video falsely claims t...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 22, 2025</span></span></span>

Investigators at MISA Lesotho’s CheckDesk fact-checking project found that the video was a deepfake. The king had never promoted the scheme. The footage had been digitally manipulated and packaged with fabricated television-style graphics designed to resemble a live news broadcast. The presentation included “breaking news” imagery and references to established media brands, creating multiple layers of apparent legitimacy around a fraudulent offer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lesotho.misa.org/2025/07/22/fact-check-viral-video-of-king-letsie-iii-promoting-investment-scheme-is-a-deepfake-scam/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lesotho.misa.org">[MISA Lesotho]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lesotho.misa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lesotho FACT-CHECK: Viral video of King Letsie III promoting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MISA LesothoFACT-CHECK: Viral video of King Letsie III promoting...July 22, 2025 — 22 Jul 2025 — A viral deepfake video falsely claims t...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 22, 2025</span></span></span>

What makes this case important is the way the deception was constructed. The scam did not rely solely on an unrealistic promise of wealth. Instead, it assembled a chain of trust:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The monarch supplied authority and familiarity.
* Fake news graphics suggested journalistic verification.
* Claims of government backing implied institutional approval.
* Advertised weekly payouts gave the impression of a structured programme rather than a speculative gamble.

</div>

Each element reinforced the others. A viewer was not being asked to trust an anonymous website; they were being encouraged to trust a face and voice they already recognised.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lesotho.misa.org/2025/07/22/fact-check-viral-video-of-king-letsie-iii-promoting-investment-scheme-is-a-deepfake-scam/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lesotho.misa.org">[MISA Lesotho]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lesotho.misa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lesotho FACT-CHECK: Viral video of King Letsie III promoting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MISA LesothoFACT-CHECK: Viral video of King Letsie III promoting...July 22, 2025 — 22 Jul 2025 — A viral deepfake video falsely claims t...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 22, 2025</span></span></span>

## The Fabricated Prime-Ministerial Interview

Only days after the exposure of the royal deepfake, fact-checkers examined another investment-related deception. This time the central figure was Prime Minister Sam Matekane. A widely shared article claimed that the prime minister had participated in a one-on-one television interview discussing a new way for Basotho citizens to earn extra income from home.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lesotho.misa.org/2025/07/30/fact-check-prime-minister-sam-matekanes-interview-allegedly-promoting-a-new-way-to-make-extra-income-is-a-hoax/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lesotho.misa.org">[MISA Lesotho]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lesotho.misa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MISA LesothoFact-check: Prime Minister Sam Matekane&#x27;s interview...30 Jul 2025 — Fact-check: Prime Minister Sam Matekane&#x27;s interview, all...</span></span></span>

The article was carefully designed to resemble a genuine media report. It referred to a real Lesotho Television presenter, Rapitso Rapitso, and described a supposedly dramatic live broadcast in which the presenter challenged the prime minister's claims. The story even alleged that the interview had later been removed from the air because of the controversy it generated. These details gave the fabricated account a sense of realism.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lesotho.misa.org/2025/07/30/fact-check-prime-minister-sam-matekanes-interview-allegedly-promoting-a-new-way-to-make-extra-income-is-a-hoax/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lesotho.misa.org">[MISA Lesotho]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lesotho.misa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MISA LesothoFact-check: Prime Minister Sam Matekane&#x27;s interview...30 Jul 2025 — Fact-check: Prime Minister Sam Matekane&#x27;s interview, all...</span></span></span>

According to MISA Lesotho's investigation, the interview never happened. The article was entirely fictional. Real photographs of Matekane and the television presenter were inserted into a fabricated narrative intended to promote a questionable money-making opportunity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lesotho.misa.org/2025/07/30/fact-check-prime-minister-sam-matekanes-interview-allegedly-promoting-a-new-way-to-make-extra-income-is-a-hoax/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lesotho.misa.org">[MISA Lesotho]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lesotho.misa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MISA LesothoFact-check: Prime Minister Sam Matekane&#x27;s interview...30 Jul 2025 — Fact-check: Prime Minister Sam Matekane&#x27;s interview, all...</span></span></span>

The structure of the hoax reveals an important evolution in financial fraud. Instead of merely attaching a celebrity photograph to an advertisement, the scammers invented an entire media event. They created the illusion that a journalist had questioned the prime minister, that a broadcaster had aired the discussion and that viewers had witnessed the exchange. The fabricated interview effectively manufactured a complete verification process that never existed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lesotho.misa.org/2025/07/30/fact-check-prime-minister-sam-matekanes-interview-allegedly-promoting-a-new-way-to-make-extra-income-is-a-hoax/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lesotho.misa.org">[MISA Lesotho]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lesotho.misa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MISA LesothoFact-check: Prime Minister Sam Matekane&#x27;s interview...30 Jul 2025 — Fact-check: Prime Minister Sam Matekane&#x27;s interview, all...</span></span></span>

## How Official Imagery Lowered Suspicion

The effectiveness of these scams depended less on technological sophistication than on social psychology. People often assess credibility by looking for familiar signals of authority. Monarchs, prime ministers, government institutions and established broadcasters function as shortcuts that help audiences decide what deserves attention. Scammers exploit those shortcuts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lesotho.misa.org/2025/07/22/fact-check-viral-video-of-king-letsie-iii-promoting-investment-scheme-is-a-deepfake-scam/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lesotho.misa.org">[MISA Lesotho]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lesotho.misa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lesotho FACT-CHECK: Viral video of King Letsie III promoting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MISA LesothoFACT-CHECK: Viral video of King Letsie III promoting...July 22, 2025 — 22 Jul 2025 — A viral deepfake video falsely claims t...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 22, 2025</span></span></span>

In the Lesotho cases, several recurring techniques appeared:

**Borrowed authority.** Real public figures were used as guarantors for offers they had never endorsed.
