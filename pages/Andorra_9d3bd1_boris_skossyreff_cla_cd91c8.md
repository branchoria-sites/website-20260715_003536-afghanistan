## Introduction

Was Boris Skossyreff ever king of [Andorra]({{ 'how-andorras-fake-king-became-a-legend/' | relative_url }})? The short answer is that he claimed to be, but the evidence does not show that he ever lawfully ruled the principality. In July 1934, the Russian-born adventurer and serial self-promoter declared himself “Boris I” during a period of political tension in Andorra. He issued proclamations, drafted a constitution and presented himself to journalists as a sovereign monarch. Yet his authority rested largely on his own declarations, disputed reports of political support and a brief burst of international publicity rather than any recognised transfer of power. Within days he was arrested by Spanish authorities acting after complaints from the Bishop of Urgell, one of Andorra’s co-princes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://h-france.net/rude/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/8-Lyons_page-proofs_final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: h-france.net">[H-France]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">h-france.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Martyn Lyons, &#x27;King Boris I of Andorra (r</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 1934) and...by M Lyons · 2025 — In July 1934, Boris Skossyreff claimed the non-existent throne of Andorra and proclaimed himself ki...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1934</span></span></span>

The enduring fascination of the episode comes from the gap between legend and documentation. Popular retellings often describe Boris as a king who successfully seized a country for a fortnight. Historians who have examined surviving records paint a more complicated picture: an impostor who exploited a constitutional grey area, attracted attention with grand promises and then became the subject of a story far larger than his actual achievements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://h-france.net/rude/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/8-Lyons_page-proofs_final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: h-france.net">[H-France]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">h-france.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Martyn Lyons, &#x27;King Boris I of Andorra (r</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 1934) and...by M Lyons · 2025 — In July 1934, Boris Skossyreff claimed the non-existent throne of Andorra and proclaimed himself ki...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1934</span></span></span>

## The Claims Boris Made About Himself

Boris Skossyreff arrived in Andorra with an impressive-sounding biography that later research found to be full of exaggerations, dubious titles and unverifiable claims. He variously portrayed himself as a member of the European aristocracy, a diplomat, a man connected to royal courts and a figure of international importance. Modern historical research has found evidence that several of these claims were false or heavily embellished, contributing to his reputation as a confidence trickster rather than a legitimate political leader.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://h-france.net/rude/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/8-Lyons_page-proofs_final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: h-france.net">[H-France]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">h-france.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Martyn Lyons, &#x27;King Boris I of Andorra (r</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 1934) and...by M Lyons · 2025 — In July 1934, Boris Skossyreff claimed the non-existent throne of Andorra and proclaimed himself ki...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1934</span></span></span>

His most ambitious claim concerned Andorra itself. Skossyreff argued that the tiny Pyrenean state needed radical modernisation. He proposed transforming it into an international financial and commercial centre, attracting foreign investment through favourable taxation and liberal economic policies. He also drafted a constitution that promised political freedoms and administrative reforms. These proposals gave his project a degree of plausibility because they addressed real debates about Andorra’s future.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://h-france.net/rude/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/8-Lyons_page-proofs_final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: h-france.net">[h-france.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">h-france.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Martyn Lyons, &#x27;King Boris I of Andorra (r</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 1934) and...by M Lyons · 2025 — In July 1934, Boris Skossyreff claimed the non-existent throne of Andorra and proclaimed himself ki...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1934</span></span></span>

After failing to persuade Andorran authorities to grant him an official role, he escalated dramatically. Operating largely from La Seu d'Urgell in neighbouring Spain rather than from within the institutions of Andorra, he proclaimed himself sovereign, adopted the title “Boris I” and announced the creation of a new political order. He issued decrees, claimed support among Andorrans and presented himself to the international press as the ruler of the country.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://h-france.net/rude/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/8-Lyons_page-proofs_final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: h-france.net">[h-france.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">h-france.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Martyn Lyons, &#x27;King Boris I of Andorra (r</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 1934) and...by M Lyons · 2025 — In July 1934, Boris Skossyreff claimed the non-existent throne of Andorra and proclaimed himself ki...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1934</span></span></span>

The performance was carefully staged. A self-proclaimed monarch issuing constitutions and declarations generated headlines far beyond what a local political dispute would normally attract. In that sense, Skossyreff's greatest success was not governing Andorra but persuading newspapers that he might be governing it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://h-france.net/rude/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/8-Lyons_page-proofs_final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: h-france.net">[H-France]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">h-france.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Martyn Lyons, &#x27;King Boris I of Andorra (r</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 1934) and...by M Lyons · 2025 — In July 1934, Boris Skossyreff claimed the non-existent throne of Andorra and proclaimed himself ki...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1934</span></span></span>

## What the Council and Co-Princes Actually Did

The central dispute concerns the extent of support Skossyreff received from Andorra's General Council, the country's representative assembly. Many later accounts repeat a dramatic story that councillors voted overwhelmingly to make him king, often citing a supposed vote of 23 to 1. The problem is that surviving evidence for this claim is uncertain and inconsistent. Historians have noted that the famous vote is much easier to find in later retellings than in clear constitutional documentation establishing a lawful monarchy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://h-france.net/rude/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/8-Lyons_page-proofs_final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: h-france.net">[H-France]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">h-france.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Martyn Lyons, &#x27;King Boris I of Andorra (r</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 1934) and...by M Lyons · 2025 — In July 1934, Boris Skossyreff claimed the non-existent throne of Andorra and proclaimed himself ki...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1934</span></span></span>

What is firmly documented is that Skossyreff had already been viewed with suspicion by authorities before his royal proclamation. Earlier attempts to influence Andorran politics had been rejected, and he had been expelled from the principality. When he returned to public attention with increasingly grand claims, the existing constitutional authorities did not recognise him as sovereign.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://h-france.net/rude/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/8-Lyons_page-proofs_final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: h-france.net">[h-france.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">h-france.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Martyn Lyons, &#x27;King Boris I of Andorra (r</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 1934) and...by M Lyons · 2025 — In July 1934, Boris Skossyreff claimed the non-existent throne of Andorra and proclaimed himself ki...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1934</span></span></span>

The decisive response came from the established system of co-princes. Since medieval times, Andorra's sovereignty had been shared between the Bishop of Urgell and the French head of state. Skossyreff's declarations directly challenged this arrangement. He even announced opposition to the Bishop of Urgell and was reported as having declared a kind of political “war” against him. The bishop responded by seeking action from Spanish authorities. Within days, Spanish police detained Skossyreff and removed him from the scene.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Skossyreff" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Boris Skossyreff</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Boris Skossyreff</span></span></span>

His arrest is one of the strongest pieces of evidence against the idea that he exercised genuine sovereignty. A ruler recognised by the state would not normally be removed so quickly by outside authorities acting at the request of one of the country's recognised co-princes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Skossyreff" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Boris Skossyreff</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Boris Skossyreff</span></span></span>

## Why the Supposed Reign Remains Disputed

The dispute survives because the episode contains just enough truth to sustain a larger myth.

Several facts are not in doubt:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Boris Skossyreff was a real person.
* He genuinely proclaimed himself ruler of Andorra.
* He produced constitutional proposals and royal declarations.
* International newspapers reported on his claims.
* He was arrested only days later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://h-france.net/rude/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/8-Lyons_page-proofs_final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: h-france.net">[h-france.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">h-france.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Martyn Lyons, &#x27;King Boris I of Andorra (r</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 1934) and...by M Lyons · 2025 — In July 1934, Boris Skossyreff claimed the non-existent throne of Andorra and proclaimed himself ki...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1934</span></span></span>

</div>

The uncertainty concerns what happened between those events. Did the General Council formally endorse him? Did some councillors merely express sympathy for his ideas? Did journalists exaggerate rumours into a constitutional revolution? ## How a Failed Bid Became a Lasting Legend

From the perspective of hoax and imposture history, Skossyreff's achievement was not the creation of a kingdom but the creation of a story. His self-coronation lasted only days, yet it generated a legend that has survived for decades. The image of an eccentric adventurer briefly ruling a European state is irresistible, and the real facts are unusual enough to keep the tale alive.

What makes the episode especially revealing is that it sits between fraud, political theatre and historical myth. Skossyreff unquestionably attempted to present himself as a legitimate monarch. At the same time, the enduring claim that he actually reigned rests on evidence that remains contested. The result is one of Andorra's most famous disputed stories: not a clear-cut hoax in which every detail was invented, but a case where a self-promoting impostor turned a failed political scheme into a remarkably durable historical legend.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://h-france.net/rude/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/8-Lyons_page-proofs_final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: h-france.net">[h-france.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">h-france.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Martyn Lyons, &#x27;King Boris I of Andorra (r</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 1934) and...by M Lyons · 2025 — In July 1934, Boris Skossyreff claimed the non-existent throne of Andorra and proclaimed himself ki...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1934</span></span></span>
