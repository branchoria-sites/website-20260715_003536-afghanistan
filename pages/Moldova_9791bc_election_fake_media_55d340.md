## Introduction

Election disinformation has become one of [Moldova]({{ 'how-false-stories-took-hold-in-moldova/' | relative_url }})’s most visible modern forms of deception. Rather than relying on forged artefacts or invented legends, recent campaigns have used manipulated videos, imitation news outlets, fake social-media personas and fabricated reports to influence how voters understand political events. The goal is often not to convince everyone of a single false story. Instead, it is to create confusion, undermine trust in reliable information and make it harder for citizens to distinguish genuine reporting from political manipulation.

Moldova’s elections have repeatedly provided examples of this strategy. From a notorious doctored video during the Chișinău mayoral race to sophisticated networks of cloned media brands and AI-generated content, the country has become a case study in how modern election misinformation operates. ## The Doctored Chișinău Election Video

One of Moldova’s most famous election misinformation incidents occurred during the 2018 mayoral campaign in Chișinău. A video circulated online that appeared to show a pro-European candidate proposing to lease the Moldovan capital to the United Arab Emirates for fifty years. For many viewers, the claim sounded outrageous but genuine because it appeared to come from the candidate’s own words.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://medium.com/dfrlab/electionwatch-doctored-video-goes-viral-ahead-of-elections-in-moldova-5d52b49f679d" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: medium.com">[Medium]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">medium.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Doctored Video Goes Viral Ahead of Elections in Moldova</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">false impression that a mayoral candidate was going to lease Chisinau to the UAE.... disinformation or “fake news”: 1. Investigators later showed that the video had been edited and paired with misleading subtitles. Researchers compared the viral version with the original interview footage and found that key elements had been altered to create a completely different meaning. The deception did not depend on sophisticated visual effects. Instead, it relied on selective editing, mistranslation and the assumption that many viewers would never see the original source material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://medium.com/dfrlab/electionwatch-doctored-video-goes-viral-ahead-of-elections-in-moldova-5d52b49f679d" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: medium.com">[Medium]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">medium.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Doctored Video Goes Viral Ahead of Elections in Moldova</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">false impression that a mayoral candidate was going to lease Chisinau to the UAE.... disinformation or “fake news”: 1. The episode is important because it illustrates a recurring feature of election misinformation. The most effective falsehoods often contain a real person, a real recording and a real event. What changes is the framing. By modifying context rather than inventing everything from scratch, propagandists create stories that feel authentic enough to spread rapidly through social networks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://medium.com/dfrlab/electionwatch-doctored-video-goes-viral-ahead-of-elections-in-moldova-5d52b49f679d" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: medium.com">[Medium]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">medium.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Doctored Video Goes Viral Ahead of Elections in Moldova</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">false impression that a mayoral candidate was going to lease Chisinau to the UAE.... disinformation or “fake news”: 1. ## AI Content and Imitation News Outlets

If the 2018 video represented an early stage of digital manipulation, later campaigns revealed a more industrialised approach. By the mid-2020s, investigators were documenting networks of fake websites designed to resemble established media organisations. These sites copied journalistic styles, logos and layouts while publishing fabricated political stories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2025/06/03/euronews-targeted-by-anti-moldova-disinformation-campaign" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: euronews.com">[euronews]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">euronews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">targeted by anti moldova disinformation campaign</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">false videos attributed to Euronews as part of the disinformation campaign.... A common tactic involved impersonating trusted international media brands. False reports presented as articles or broadcasts from well-known outlets circulated on Telegram, Facebook and other platforms. Some fabricated stories targeted individual politicians, while others promoted broader narratives about corruption, foreign influence or Moldova’s geopolitical direction. Fact-checkers repeatedly found that the supposed reports had never been published by the media organisations whose names appeared on the material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2025/06/03/euronews-targeted-by-anti-moldova-disinformation-campaign" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: euronews.com">[euronews]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">euronews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">targeted by anti moldova disinformation campaign</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">false videos attributed to Euronews as part of the disinformation campaign.... Researchers also observed increasing use of artificial intelligence. According to monitoring groups and international reporting, AI tools were employed to generate fake personas, realistic profile photographs, automated comments and large volumes of political content. These techniques reduced the cost of running influence campaigns and allowed operators to create the illusion of widespread public support or opposition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.euronews.com/next/2025/09/23/inside-russias-ai-driven-disinformation-machine-shaping-moldovas-election" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: euronews.com">[euronews.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">euronews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">inside russias ai driven disinformation machine shaping moldovas election</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">inside russias ai driven disinformation machine shaping moldovas election</span></span></span>

Several investigations described networks of AI-assisted social-media accounts posting coordinated messages, amplifying identical narratives and attempting to push election-related topics into recommendation systems. ## The Role of Fake Accounts and Coordinated Identities

Election disinformation rarely succeeds because of a single viral post. More often, it spreads through coordinated networks that make false stories appear popular.

Monitoring organisations studying Moldovan elections identified clusters of accounts that shared the same material, repeated identical talking points and interacted with one another in ways suggesting central coordination. Some networks used hundreds of accounts; others relied on smaller groups that amplified messages across several platforms simultaneously.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://edmo.eu/publications/analysis-of-coordinated-inauthentic-behavior-in-moldova-23-days-before-the-elections/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: edmo.eu">[EDMO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">edmo.eu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Analysis of coordinated inauthentic behavior in Moldova</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Analysis of coordinated inauthentic behavior in Moldova</span></span></span>

Researchers examining activity before major elections found examples of fake accounts presenting themselves as ordinary voters while promoting highly similar political narratives. Some used AI-generated profile images and fabricated biographies. Others attempted to mimic local users by adopting familiar names, regional references or everyday social content alongside political messaging.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://edmo.eu/publications/analysis-of-coordinated-inauthentic-behavior-in-moldova-23-days-before-the-elections/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: edmo.eu">[EDMO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">edmo.eu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Analysis of coordinated inauthentic behavior in Moldova</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Analysis of coordinated inauthentic behavior in Moldova</span></span></span>

The effect was less about persuading citizens through careful argument than creating the appearance of consensus. When large numbers of accounts repeat the same claim, users may interpret that repetition as evidence that many people independently believe it. In reality, the apparent agreement can be artificially manufactured.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://promolex.md/en/massive-online-networks-that-manipulate-voting/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: promolex.md">[Promo-LEX]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">promolex.md</span><span class="citation-popover-title">massive online networks that manipulate voting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">massive online networks that manipulate voting</span></span></span>

## Why Confusion Can Matter More Than Persuasion

A common misunderstanding is that election disinformation succeeds only when people completely believe a fabricated story. In practice, many campaigns appear designed to achieve something broader: uncertainty.

When voters encounter a constant stream of conflicting reports, altered videos, fake news articles and competing accusations, trust in information itself can begin to erode. Citizens may stop believing particular stories, but they may also stop believing corrections, journalists or institutions. ## What These Campaigns Reveal About Modern Political Hoaxes

Moldova’s election misinformation campaigns show how the classic logic of a hoax survives in a digital age. The underlying formula is familiar: borrow credibility from a trusted source, alter or fabricate information, distribute it widely and rely on emotion to outrun verification.

Today, a manipulated video, cloned news site or AI-generated persona can reach millions of users through social platforms within hours.
